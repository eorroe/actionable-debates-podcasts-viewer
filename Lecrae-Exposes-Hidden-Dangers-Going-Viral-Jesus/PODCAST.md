# Lecrae Exposes Hidden Dangers of Going Viral for Jesus

## Overview

This episode of The Deep End with Lecrae features a raw, unscripted conversation about the dark side of the Christian content creation world. Lecrae and Bryce Crawford expose how Christianity has shifted from a lifestyle to a cultural label, and why going viral for Jesus carries hidden spiritual dangers—including faith deconstruction (the process of critically questioning and often abandoning previously held religious beliefs), inauthenticity, and the loss of genuine faith among creators.

## When to Follow Podcast

- When you want to understand the hidden dangers of Christian content creation and going viral for Jesus
- When wrestling with the difference between authentic Christianity and performative religious branding
- When considering how to share your faith online without losing your own spiritual life
- When reflecting on street evangelism versus relational evangelism as outreach strategies


## Discussed

### The Hidden Dangers of Going Viral for Jesus

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Recognize that Christianity has become a label, not a lifestyle

Christianity is increasingly treated as a cultural brand or identity marker rather than a lived, transformative walk with God. This shift makes it easy for people to adopt the label without the substance, and for creators to build audiences around the label alone.

##### Step 2: Be aware that Christian content creation can be a pipeline to deconstruction

Lecrae notes that people who entered Christian social media "in any capacity" have since abandoned the faith. The environment of performative Christianity can ironically lead people away from it.

##### Step 3: Examine your motives for creating Christian content

Before posting or going viral, ask why you are creating. Is it to genuinely point people to Jesus, or is it driven by the platform, clout, or the feeling that being saved now means you "got to go make Christian content"?

##### Step 4: Prioritize genuine relationships over viral reach

Lecrae and Bryce Crawford's experience shows that the highest conversion rates come through relationships, not street evangelism or viral content. Invest in real, long-term connections rather than chasing the largest audience.

##### Step 5: Guard your own spiritual life while being public about your faith

Being "unashamed of the gospel" (Romans 1:16) is a calling, but it must be rooted in sincerity—not performance. The pressure of a public Christian identity can erode private faith if not carefully guarded.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Burning Man Encounter

Bryce Crawford, an attendee, was told, "You're saved? So now you got to go make Christian content" after attending Burning Man. This illustrates the cultural pressure to immediately convert one's faith story into content—a pressure that can feel like obligation rather than calling.

##### Example 2: The Deconstruction Pipeline

When Lecrae first became a Christian, he befriended people doing Christian social media in every capacity. Half of them are no longer Christians. This demonstrates the real, documented spiritual cost for those who built their identity around Christian content rather than Christ Himself.

##### Example 3: Bryce Crawford, the 21-Year-Old Street Evangelist

Bryce Crawford shares that his prayer when starting to date was, "God, can I take your daughter out on a date?"—showing a sincere, submitted approach to life. Yet he chooses street evangelism despite its notoriously low conversion rates, raising questions about how to measure faithfulness versus effectiveness in outreach.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Root your online presence in genuine discipleship, not just content production
- ✅ Measure spiritual success by transformed lives and deep relationships, not views or follows
- ✅ Hold your faith loosely enough to examine it, but tightly enough to live it—avoid making Christianity just content
- ✅ Seek accountability from people who know the real you, not just your online persona
- ✅ Let your life be the primary witness; use content as a supplement, not a substitute
- ✅ Remember Romans 1:16—be unashamed of the gospel, but be unashamed of the *person* of Christ, not the performance of Christianity

#### Keep In Mind

- Christianity has become a popular cultural label. Being identified as a Christian now carries social currency (social prestige or cultural capital), which changes the incentive structure for content creation.
- The highest rate of conversion comes through relationships, not mass evangelism or viral content. Building trust over time is more effective than reaching the most people.
- Authenticity is your greatest asset. Lecrae is drawn to Bryce Crawford because of his sincere passion for Jesus and willingness to engage people at festivals like Burning Man and in the rap scene—contexts where Christianity is often least expected.
- Young creators (like Bryce Crawford) are entering the space with wisdom beyond their years. Protect and invest in them, but also challenge them to avoid burnout and inauthenticity.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Public Christian identity exposes creators to scrutiny, criticism, and manipulation. Be prepared for both spiritual and emotional attacks from those who disagree with your message.
- The deconstruction risk is real: Christian content creators who built their identity around content rather than Christ lose their faith. Regular personal spiritual disciplines (prayer, Scripture, community) are essential to prevent this.
- Avoid treating your testimony as a commodity. Sharing vulnerable parts of your story for clicks can lead to exploitation of your own spiritual journey.
- Be cautious of platforms and communities that reward outrage or controversy over genuine gospel witness.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating Christianity as content rather than a calling, leading to burnout or loss of faith.
  **Solution:** Establish clear boundaries between your personal spiritual life and your public content. Keep some parts of your walk private.

- **Problem:** Measuring success by views, follows, or viral reach rather than by genuine spiritual impact.
  **Solution:** Set metrics based on discipleship, community health, and transformed lives—not analytics.

- **Problem:** Chasing the hardest evangelism methods (like street evangelism) while neglecting the most effective ones (deep, long-term relationships).
  **Solution:** Evaluate your methods against real outcomes. Let effectiveness, not just effort or visibility, guide your strategy.

- **Problem:** Allowing Christianity to become a label or brand that separates you from the very people you are called to reach.
  **Solution:** Engage people in their context—at Burning Man, in the rap scene, wherever they are—rather than building a separate Christian cultural bubble.

### The Depth of Authentic Faith in a Public Space

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Embrace being unashamed of the gospel without being performative about it

Romans 1:16 calls believers to be unashamed, but this confidence should flow from a genuine relationship with Christ, not from the platform or performance. Authenticity resonates more than polished religious content.

##### Step 2: Engage people where they actually are

Lecrae is noted for going to places like Burning Man and engaging rappers and unexpected audiences. Do not limit your witness to Christian spaces—go into the world, not just into Christian culture.

##### Step 3: Keep a childlike dependence on God

Lecrae's reaction—"Yo, God, protect this man. He's too wise for his age"—reflects the weight of calling young ministers like Bryce Crawford into public ministry. Cultivate humility and dependence on God, not self-reliance or platform confidence.

##### Step 4: Ground dating and relationships in prayer

Bryce Crawford's practice of praying, "God, can I take your daughter out on a date?" models submitting personal life decisions to God. Let your relationships be a testimony, not a scandal or distraction.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Lecrae at Burning Man and With Rappers

Lecrae's willingness to show up at Burning Man and engage rappers—audiences far outside typical Christian circles—demonstrates incarnational ministry (meeting people where they are rather than expecting them to come to him).

##### Example 2: Bryce Crawford's Prayerful Approach to Dating

When asked about dating, Bryce Crawford immediately frames it through prayer and submission to God: "God, can I take your daughter out on a date?" This reflects a worldview in which every decision is submitted to God, not just the religious ones.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Let your life—not just your content—be your witness
- ✅ Engage culture rather than retreating from it
- ✅ Submit personal decisions (dating, finances, career) to prayer, not just public ministry decisions
- ✅ Stay humble and teachable even as your platform grows
- ✅ Let sincerity and risk-taking mark your ministry, not safety and conformity

#### Keep In Mind

- Lecrae's protective prayer over Bryce Crawford—"God, protect this man. He's too wise for his age"—reminds us that wisdom and visibility can be a heavy burden, especially for the young.
- Lecrae's authenticity and risk-taking have made him a trusted voice across generations. This kind of trust takes years to build and can be lost quickly through inauthenticity.
- Being "unashamed of the gospel" is not the same as being unashamed of Christianity as a cultural product. Make sure you are pointing people to Jesus, not to your brand or platform.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Public ministry exposes you to spiritual opposition (spiritual warfare—resistance or hostility directed at advancing the gospel in unconventional spaces).
- Young, gifted, and public ministers (like Bryce Crawford) are especially vulnerable to pride, burnout, and compromise. Surround yourself with mature accountability.
- Protect your testimony by keeping your personal life aligned with your public message.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Building a platform that becomes more important than the person you are pointing to.
  **Solution:** Regularly ask yourself: if I lost my platform tomorrow, would I still follow Jesus? Let the answer determine your health.

- **Problem:** Letting the pressure of public expectation shape your message rather than the Holy Spirit.
  **Solution:** Maintain silence, solitude, and Scripture before you speak to crowds. Let God shape your words before the world does.

- **Problem:** Assuming that visibility equals effectiveness. Viral content does not equal converted hearts.
  **Solution:** Track spiritual fruit in people's lives, not impressions. The world does not need more Christian content—it needs more Christians who live like Christ.
