# Cliffe Knechtle's Atheist Debates Ever!

## Overview

A compilation of live debates between Cliffe Knechtle and atheists, ex-Christians, and students on university campuses, addressing objections to Christianity through questions of morality (e.g., "Why is date rape wrong?"; "Why does a zebra deserve to have its throat crushed by a lion?"), the Bible (e.g., contradictions in gospel accounts; slavery and homosexuality in the Bible), Jesus as the only way (e.g., "Isn't Gandhi burning in hell?"), hell (e.g., "I don't want to go to heaven"), the resurrection (e.g., "Where is the evidence that Jesus physically rose from the dead?"), suffering (e.g., "Why do babies suffer?"), science and faith (e.g., "God is energy"; "I didn't feel anything when I was a Christian"), original sin (e.g., "I was born with this desire, therefore it is good"), and homosexuality (e.g., "Which one is for everyone for eternity and this one's just for you guys right now?"). The debates model how to engage challengers with grace, intellectual honesty, and a commitment to evidence-based dialogue.

## When to Follow Debate

- When you need to defend Christianity against objections from atheists or skeptics
- When working through questions about morality, suffering, the Bible, or Jesus as the only way
- When the user asks how to engage in dialogue about faith with intellectual respect and clarity, asking hard questions while refusing to water down core Christian claims

## Glossary / Prerequisite Terms To Know Before Following Debate

- **Hell**: The consequence of freely choosing to be separated from God forever, not arbitrary torture. Cliffe argues that God does not send people to hell; they choose it by rejecting him.
- **Heaven**: Cliffe defines eternal life as knowing God and Jesus Christ (John 17:3), involving real relationship with God and others, meaningful work, creativity, and service. Not endless worship or mindless praise.
- **Suffering**: Moral evil (human choices like rape, murder) and natural evil (disease, natural disasters) resulting from a world broken by human rebellion in Genesis 3. Animals suffer because the whole created order is broken, not because they sinned.
- **Homosexuality**: In the context of these debates, Cliffe argues the Bible's view is rooted in Genesis 1-2 (creation of male and female for lifelong marriage) and the New Testament's consistent teaching, not Levitical ceremonial law.
- **Theocracy**: A form of government in which God is recognized as the supreme civil ruler, applied specifically to the ancient nation of Israel.
- **Ceremonial law**: The laws governing worship, sacrifices, purity, and religious practices in ancient Israel (e.g., dietary restrictions, circumcision), distinct from moral law.
- **Civil law**: The laws governing the nation of Israel's civil life (e.g., property laws, penalties for crimes), distinct from moral law.
- **Moral law**: The universal, timeless moral principles reflecting God's character (e.g., the Ten Commandments in Exodus 20), binding on all people for all time.
- **Inerrancy**: The claim that the Bible contains no errors in its original autographs. In these debates, the term is raised by skeptics, and Cliffe acknowledges textual variants while arguing no doctrine depends on them.
- **Polygamy**: The practice of having multiple spouses. Cliffe cites Solomon's 700 wives and 300 concubines as an example of deviation from God's design, not an endorsement.
- **Crusades**: Military campaigns sanctioned by the Catholic Church during the medieval period, cited in the debates as historical evil done in Christianity's name.
- **Inquisition**: Historical institutions within the Catholic Church tasked with identifying and punishing heresy, cited in the debates as historical evil done in Christianity's name.
- **Salem Witch Trials**: 1692 trials in colonial Massachusetts where people were executed for witchcraft, cited in the debates as historical evil done in Christianity's name.
- **Slavery**: The practice of owning human beings as property. In the debates, Cliffe acknowledges the Mosaic legislation regulated rather than abolished slavery, and notes Christians both enslaved people and led the abolition movement.
- **Caste system**: The Hindu social hierarchy where people are born into different castes considered to have different values, contrasted with Christianity where all are created in God's image.
- **Sermon on the Mount**: Jesus' ethical teachings in Matthew 5-7, described by Harvard psychology professor Robert Kohl as the greatest ethical document ever written.
- **Date rape**: Sexual assault by someone known to the victim, used in the debates as an example of a moral absolute that people recognize as wrong.
- **Original sin**: The readiness to sin factor present in all humans from birth, not imputed guilt but a natural inclination toward wrongdoing
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: A doctrine that blames individuals for the actions of Adam and Eve, seen as unjust punishment for a crime they did not personally commit
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: A spiritual birth defect and inherent tendency toward sin that all humans inherit, not God-given but arising from human rebellion in Genesis 3
- **Mosaic Legislation**: The 613 laws given to the nation of Israel in Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy, governing the theocracy of ancient Israel
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: Distinguished from the moral law (Exodus 20), with the ceremonial and civil laws applying specifically to Israel as a nation and not binding on all people for all time
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: Part of a larger set of biblical rules, with no clear distinction made within the text between temporary and eternal laws
- **Naturalism**: The philosophical presupposition that reality is limited to matter and energy, excluding the supernatural
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: The default position for evaluating historical and scientific claims, requiring natural explanations for all events
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: A presupposition that filters out the possibility of miracles and the resurrection before examining the historical evidence
- **Free Will**: The ability to make genuine choices, including the choice to love or reject God and others
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: Given by God to humans, allowing real love and real rebellion, and the basis for moral responsibility
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: Can be explained through natural processes, and raises questions about why a good God would grant free will if it leads to suffering
- **Resurrection**: The claim that Jesus physically rose from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: The central historical event validating Jesus' claims, supported by eyewitness testimony and the transformation of the early church
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: A supernatural claim requiring extraordinary evidence, considered philosophically impossible under naturalism
- **Manuscript Variants**: Differences between ancient copies of biblical manuscripts, such as whether a detail is mentioned once or twice
  - **From Christian Viewpoint**: So small and insignificant that no doctrine depends on any single variant, demonstrating the remarkable reliability of the New Testament text
  - **From Atheist Viewpoint**: Evidence that the Bible is a human document with transmission errors, undermining claims of inerrancy
- **Materialism**: The worldview that physical matter is the only reality, and that all phenomena can be explained by material causes. In the debates, Cliffe contrasts this with Christianity, arguing that living for money and material possessions is an empty life.
- **Karma**: The Hindu concept that actions in this life determine one's fate in future lives. In the debates, a challenger mentions karma as a non-Christian basis for moral judgment.
- **Grace**: In Christianity, the unmerited favor of God toward sinners, forgiveness and new life through Jesus Christ, not earned by good works.
- **Presupposition**: A prior assumption or commitment that shapes how one evaluates evidence. In the debates, Cliffe argues that naturalism is a presupposition that rules out the supernatural before examining the evidence.
- **Immaterial**: Not physical or material; in the debates, used to describe love, moral values, and beauty as real but not measurable by physical instruments.
- **Transcendent**: Going beyond the physical or material; in the debates, used to describe moral values and love as real and not merely chemical reactions or social conventions.
- **Moral obligation**: A duty to act based on moral principles; in the debates, used to describe why Christians share their faith.


## Discussed Topics

### Christianity vs Other Religions

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Why Christianity over Islam or Hinduism?

Cliffe argues that all world religions make contradictory truth claims. Muhammad's claim that Jesus was merely a good prophet contradicts Jesus' own claim to be God. Since Muhammad never met Jesus and lived 500 years after him, Cliffe says you cannot trust him as a primary source over the eyewitnesses who lived with Jesus. Islam's internal contradictions and Hinduism's caste system are cited as reasons to reject those religions in favor of Christianity.

##### Example 2: Evidence-Based Trust

Trusting Jesus is not based on Cliffe's passion or sincerity. Instead, the evidence of Jesus' sinless life, his ethical teachings (the Sermon on the Mount), his death on the cross forgiving his enemies, and his resurrection from the dead is presented as the reason to trust him. If someone dies and rises from the dead, that is evidence they are trustworthy.

##### Example 3: Study Two Serious Options

You do not need to study every religion. Take the two or three that seem most credible and examine them: read the Gospels as history, read the Quran, ask whether the evidence points to Jesus or Muhammad being reliable. Do not assume all religions are equal - they make contradictory claims about God, heaven, and salvation.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish between Christianity as a human institution and Jesus Christ as the reliable founder
- ✅ Examine the historical evidence of how Jesus lived, taught, died, and rose from the dead
- ✅ Read competing religious texts (Gospels, Quran, etc.) as history, not just as inspired Scripture
- ✅ Compare the internal consistency and character of each religion's founder
- ✅ Ask hard questions of every worldview, including your own
- ❌ Do not put your faith in Christianity as an institution
- ❌ Do not trust Jesus simply because someone else is passionate about him
- ❌ Do not assume all religions are equally valid or teach the same truth
- ❌ Do not use the failures of Christians to justify rejecting Christ

#### Keep In Mind

- Cliffe argues that religions make mutually exclusive truth claims and therefore cannot all be true.
- Jesus' claim to be God is the critical dividing line. He is either a liar, a lunatic, or Lord.
- Muhammad lived 500 years after Jesus and never met him. The eyewitnesses to Jesus (Matthew, Mark, John, Peter, James) are the primary sources for who Jesus claimed to be.
- Even if Christianity as an institution has been used to justify evil (Crusades, Inquisition, slavery), Jesus himself is distinct from his followers.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not conflate Christianity as a cultural or political institution with the person and claims of Jesus Christ.
- When engaging with someone from another faith, approach with genuine love and a desire to understand why they believe what they believe.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Rejecting Christianity because of hypocrisy in the church or the use of Christianity to justify historical evils
  **Solution:** Separate Jesus from Christians. Evaluate Jesus based on his own life, teachings, death, and resurrection.
- **Problem:** Assuming all religions are equally valid because they all seem to teach love
  **Solution:** Note that religions make contradictory claims about God, salvation, and the afterlife. They cannot all be true.
- **Problem:** Trusting Jesus simply because a believer is passionate or sincere
  **Solution:** Faith must be grounded in the credibility of its object. Read the Gospels and examine the evidence yourself.


### Christianity vs Other Religions

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Muhammad Contradicts Jesus

Cliffe argues that Muhammad's claim that Jesus was a great prophet but not God contradicts the eyewitness testimony of those who lived with Jesus for three years and heard him claim to be God. Since Muhammad never met Jesus and lived 500 years later, he is not a reliable primary source on who Jesus really was.

##### Example 2: Materialism as a Religion

Cliffe observes that most of his friends are materialists who work on Wall Street (Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan) and are very good at making money. He is convinced that putting ultimate faith in materialism is a mistake. Money is good but living for it is not. Christianity offers a better alternative in Christ.

##### Example 3: The Trustworthiness Test

If someone rose from the dead, you should listen carefully to everything they say because the evidence is they are reliable. Jesus Christ is the only figure in history who made this claim and whose followers testified to its fulfillment.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Test every religion and worldview against the evidence of its founder's life, character, and historical claims
- ✅ Be honest about your own cultural and religious biases
- ✅ Love your friends enough to read their religious texts and understand their beliefs
- ✅ Be willing to change your mind if the evidence points to a different conclusion
- ❌ Do not claim to have studied all religions equally - it is impossible
- ❌ Do not confuse sincerity or passion with truth
- ❌ Do not assume that because Christianity has been misused, Jesus himself is not trustworthy
- ❌ Do not adopt naturalism as an unquestionable presupposition that rules out miracles before examining the evidence

#### Keep In Mind

- Open-mindedness is not cynicism. You cannot study every religion equally, but you must follow the evidence where it leads.
- Cliffe presents the Lewis trilemma: if Jesus is not God, he is either a liar or a lunatic, with no middle ground of "just a good moral teacher."
- Cliffe treats the resurrection as a historical claim that can be evaluated like other historical events.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When someone shares their deconversion story (e.g., "I didn't feel anything when I was a Christian, so I turned away from it"), ask what standard they were using. Were they expecting to feel God's love as an emotional experience rather than examining the historical evidence of Jesus' life, teachings, death, and resurrection?
- Do not dismiss someone's doubt or pain. Engage honestly with the reasons they stopped believing.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Requiring a feeling of God's love as proof of God's existence
  **Solution:** Faith is based on the credibility of Jesus, not on subjective feelings. Examine the historical evidence.
- **Problem:** Believing that because someone sincerely holds a belief, it must be true
  **Solution:** Sincerity does not make a belief true. Walking on thin ice with sincere faith will not keep you from falling through.


### Morality - The Basis of Good and Evil

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Where Do Moral Absolutes Come From?

An atheist says date rape is wrong, but when asked why, cannot ground that moral conviction in naturalism. If there is no God, good and evil are just social conventions, power structures, or chemical reactions. But if there is a God who created us in his image, there is an objective basis for morality that transcends individual or cultural opinion.

##### Example 2: Morality Is Not Based on Feelings

Cliffe argues that his own conscience convicts him that he does wrong even when he knows what is right. This internal experience of guilt points to a real, objective moral law that we all violate. The question is not whether atheists can be moral, but how they account for objective moral values if God does not exist.

##### Example 3: Goodness Requires a Mind

Goodness is an intangible value that requires a mind to define it. A tree or a cement block has no understanding of good. If goodness exists as an objective reality, it must be grounded in a mind. If there is no God, goodness is not eternal because it requires a mind to define it.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Ask "why is that wrong?" when someone asserts a moral claim
- ✅ Distinguish between moral behavior and the foundation for morality
- ✅ Point to universal moral intuitions (e.g., torturing babies for fun is wrong) as evidence for objective morality
- ✅ Acknowledge that atheists can and do live moral lives
- ❌ Do not claim that atheists have no morals
- ❌ Do not reduce morality to religious rules without explaining the objective basis
- ❌ Do not dismiss the question of evil without giving an answer

#### Keep In Mind

- The question is not "Can atheists be moral?" but "How do atheists account for objective morality?"
- Everyone experiences guilt, which points to a real moral standard we fail to meet.
- Cliffe argues that if there is no God, there is no objective good or evil - only personal preference, cultural convention, or evolutionary conditioning.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Be careful not to use the problem of evil as a bludgeon. Acknowledge honestly that no one can give a complete answer to why God allows suffering.
- When discussing hell, do not claim to know who is or is not there. Only God judges.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Demanding that God prevent all evil and suffering
  **Solution:** If God gives humans free will, then moral evil (rape, murder, etc.) is the result of human choices, not God's failure. Cliffe teaches that natural evil is a consequence of the world being broken by human rebellion in Genesis 3.
- **Problem:** Arguing that atheists have no basis for morality
  **Solution:** Affirm that atheists can and do act morally. The question is the foundation: without God, moral values are subjective and relative.


### The Bible and Its Reliability

#### Examples

##### Example 1: No Contradictions in the Bible

When critics point to differences between gospel accounts (e.g., one angel at the tomb vs. two), Cliffe argues these are not contradictions but different perspectives, like two eyewitnesses to the same car accident reporting different details. If all four gospels said exactly the same thing, it would look like collusion.

##### Example 2: Distinguishing Mosaic Law from Moral Law

Cliffe points out that there is a humongous difference between the 613 ceremonial and civil laws for the theocracy of Israel (Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy) and the moral law of God (Exodus 20). Confusing them leads to absurd conclusions. Christians are not bound by the ceremonial laws (dietary restrictions, sacrifices) but are bound by the moral law (do not murder, do not steal, etc.).

##### Example 3: Manuscript Variants

Cliffe notes that there are textual variants in the 5,200 Greek manuscripts of the New Testament, but they are so small that no doctrine depends on them. The variant in Matthew 1 about whether "Jesus was the son of Mary, a virgin" is stated once or twice does not change any doctrine.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish between the Mosaic theocratic legislation and the universal moral law
- ✅ Understand that gospel differences reflect different eyewitness perspectives, not contradictions
- ✅ Know the facts about New Testament manuscript evidence
- ✅ Be honest about textual variants while explaining their insignificance
- ❌ Do not defend the Crusades, Inquisition, or slavery as Christian acts
- ❌ Do not claim the Bible has zero textual problems
- ❌ Do not use Old Testament civil laws to condemn modern practices without understanding their context
- ❌ Do not use Leviticus to condemn homosexuality without explaining the full biblical argument from Genesis and the New Testament

#### Keep In Mind

- Mark Twain said it is not the parts of the Bible he does not understand that disturb him, but the parts he does understand.
- The Bible is simple and straightforward on the basics: love God, love your neighbor, do not murder, do not steal, etc.
- Christians have repeatedly misinterpreted the Bible to justify evil. This does not invalidate the Bible's teachings, but it does require careful exegesis.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When someone points to Christian hypocrisy or historical evil done in Christianity's name, do not defend the evil. Acknowledge it, condemn it, and point to Jesus himself.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using Old Testament ceremonial laws (Leviticus) to condemn modern practices without distinguishing them from the moral law
  **Solution:** Explain the distinction between the theocracy of Israel and the moral law. The case against homosexuality is based on Genesis 1-2 and the New Testament, not on Levitical ceremonial law.
- **Problem:** Defending the Inquisition, Crusades, or slavery by quoting the Bible
  **Solution:** These are abuses of Christianity, not expressions of true Christianity. Evaluate Jesus, not Christians.


### Jesus as the Only Way

#### Examples

##### Example 1: John 14:6 - The Exclusive Claim

Jesus said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." This is an exclusive claim. Either Jesus is who he said he is, or he is a liar or a lunatic. There is no middle ground of "just a good moral teacher."

##### Example 2: Judgment Belongs to God Alone

Cliffe says he does not know whether Gandhi is in heaven or hell. He refuses to judge any individual's eternal destiny because only God knows what a person decides before they die. Christians should not sit in judgment on who is saved and who is not.

##### Example 3: Christians Who Do Evil Will Face Judgment

If a Christian enslaves people, rapes, or murders, they have violated Christ's teachings. Cliffe does not know whether such people are genuine followers of Christ. If they are genuine, they will face judgment. If they are not genuine, they are false Christians.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Affirm Jesus' exclusive claim while refusing to judge individual eternal destinies
- ✅ Acknowledge the existence of Christian hypocrisy and evil done in Christ's name
- ✅ Distinguish between genuine followers of Christ and cultural or nominal Christians
- ✅ Do not claim certainty about who is in heaven or hell
- ❌ Do not claim that all who call themselves Christians are truly saved
- ❌ Do not claim certainty about the eternal destiny of any specific individual
- ❌ Do not use John 14:6 as a club to condemn people

#### Keep In Mind

- Every person who makes a truth claim excludes contradictory claims. This is not intolerance; it is the nature of truth.
- Gandhi is respected, but if he rejected Christ, the same standard applies to him as to any other person.
- The fact that Christians have done evil does not invalidate Christ's claims.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim to know who is or is not going to heaven. God alone is judge.
- When someone raises the problem of Christian hypocrisy, acknowledge it honestly rather than defending it.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using Jesus' exclusive claim to condemn people
  **Solution:** State the truth claim but leave judgment to God. Offer the gospel as good news, not as condemnation.
- **Problem:** Assuming all who call themselves Christians are genuine followers of Christ
  **Solution:** Distinguish between cultural Christianity and genuine discipleship. Jesus warned that those who call him Lord will be rejected.


### Hell and Heaven

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Why Does Hell Exist?

Cliffe explains that hell exists because God gives humans free will. If God is all-powerful but chooses to limit his power by giving us free will, then we are responsible for our own choices. God did not force anyone to reject him. Hell is the consequence of freely choosing to be separated from God forever.

##### Example 2: Heaven Is About Relationship and Work

Heaven is not just endless worship. It is about relationship with God and with others, and about meaningful work. Cliffe cites John 17:3 to define eternal life as knowing God and Jesus Christ. Work is a gift from God, not a curse. In heaven, we will continue to create, serve, and love.

##### Example 3: The Problem of Eternal Punishment

An atheist student objects that 80 years of finite life does not deserve infinite punishment. Cliffe acknowledges the tension but points to the gravity of rebelling against an infinite God. The punishment is not arbitrary; it is the natural consequence of choosing to be forever separated from the source of all good.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Explain hell as the natural consequence of freely rejecting God, not as arbitrary torture
- ✅ Present heaven as relationship and work, not just worship
- ✅ Acknowledge that people find the doctrine challenging
- ❌ Do not delight in the idea of hell
- ❌ Do not claim certainty about who is in hell
- ❌ Do not reduce heaven to a place of endless leisure or worship only

#### Keep In Mind

- Hell exists because God respects human free will. He does not force anyone to love him.
- Heaven is primarily about knowing God and Jesus Christ (John 17:3).
- Both heaven and hell appeal to different people. Cliffe suggests that some people reject heaven because they do not want to surrender to God's authority.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim to know the eternal destiny of any person, including yourself.
- When someone says they do not want heaven, explore why. Is it because of a misunderstanding of what heaven is?

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Describing hell as God torturing people arbitrarily
  **Solution:** Hell is the consequence of freely choosing to be separated from God. Cliffe argues that God does not send people to hell; they choose it by rejecting him.
- **Problem:** Reducing Christianity to a fire insurance policy
  **Solution:** Cliffe emphasizes that Christianity is about relationship with God, not just avoiding hell.


### The Resurrection and Evidence for Christianity

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Empty Tomb and Changed Worship

Cliffe claims that, because Jesus rose on the first day, this is evidence that something extraordinary happened to convince them.

##### Example 2: The Credibility of the Witnesses

Cliffe claims that eyewitnesses saw the risen Jesus and were willing to die for what they saw. Cliffe argues that if the resurrection were a lie, the disciples would have recanted under persecution. Instead, they died for their testimony.

##### Example 3: The Falsifiability of Christ's Claim

Jesus claimed he would die and rise from the dead. This made his claim falsifiable. If he did not rise, he is a liar. If he did rise, he is the truth. The evidence of the empty tomb and the transformed lives of the disciples points to the resurrection.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Read the Gospels as history, not just as inspired Scripture
- ✅ Examine the evidence of Jesus' life, teachings, death, and resurrection
- ✅ Ask whether the historical evidence points to Jesus being reliable
- ❌ Do not believe in Jesus simply because someone else is passionate
- ❌ Do not base your faith on feelings or subjective experiences
- ❌ Do not dismiss the resurrection as impossible before examining the historical evidence

#### Keep In Mind

- Cliffe defines credibility as the reliability of the object of faith based on historical evidence. Faith is not determined by passion or sincerity; it is determined by the credibility of the object of faith.
- If you sincerely believe in thin ice and walk on it, you will fall through. The object of your faith must be reliable.
- 1 Corinthians 15: If Christ is not risen, Christians are idiots for believing in him.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim that believing in Jesus will make you feel good or solve all your problems.
- Do not dismiss someone's doubt or lack of feeling as a failure on their part.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing in Jesus because of a passionate preacher or emotional experience
  **Solution:** Read the Gospels yourself. Examine the historical evidence. Ask whether Jesus is trustworthy based on his own claims and resurrection.
- **Problem:** Rejecting the resurrection because you are a naturalist who rules out the supernatural before examining the evidence
  **Solution:** Do not let philosophical naturalism determine historical conclusions. Examine the evidence on its own terms.


### Science, Faith, and Naturalism

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Supernatural Is Not Anti-Science

A religious studies major says she does not believe in the resurrection because she is a naturalist. Cliffe argues this is a philosophical presupposition, not a scientific conclusion. If you rule out the supernatural before examining the evidence, you are not doing honest history; you are doing naturalistic philosophy.

##### Example 2: Love Is Not Just Chemistry

An atheist says love is just a chemical reaction. Cliffe argues that love is a real, intangible value that cannot be reduced to matter and energy. If you experience love from others, you know there is more to reality than the material. This points to a God who created love as a real, transcendent value.

##### Example 3: God Is a Personal Being, Not Energy

When someone says God is love or God is energy, Cliffe pushes back. God is a personal being with a mind, not an impersonal force. Love is a free decision to care about someone, not a physical energy. If God were just energy or love as a concept, he would not be a personal being who created us for relationship.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish between philosophical naturalism and scientific methodology
- ✅ Argue that love, moral values, and beauty are real but immaterial
- ✅ Ask whether the atheist's commitment to naturalism is based on evidence or presupposition
- ❌ Do not reduce God to an impersonal force or energy
- ❌ Do not conflate scientific method with philosophical naturalism
- ❌ Do not claim that science disproves God

#### Keep In Mind

- Science studies the natural world. It cannot answer questions about God, morality, or meaning.
- The resurrection is a historical claim, not a scientific one. It can be evaluated by historical methods.
- Cliffe argues that if there is no God, then love, good, and evil are just chemical reactions or social conventions. But we experience them as real, transcendent values.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim that science and faith are inherently in conflict. They address different questions.
- When someone says they turned away from Christianity because they did not feel anything, ask what standard they were using.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Accepting naturalism as the default position without examining its philosophical basis
  **Solution:** Ask whether the rejection of the supernatural is based on evidence or on a prior commitment to materialism.
- **Problem:** Reducing God to a vague spiritual energy or concept
  **Solution:** God is a personal being who created us for relationship, not an impersonal force.


### Suffering and the Problem of Evil

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Free Will Explains Human Evil

If God is all-powerful and all-loving, why does evil exist? The answer is free will. God limited his power by giving humans genuine freedom to love or hate, to do good or evil. When people choose evil, they are exercising the free will God gave them. God is not responsible for human choices.

##### Example 2: The Fall and a Broken World

Genesis 1-2 records a good creation. Genesis 3 records human rebellion against God. When humans told God to leave, God honored their free will and stepped back. The result is a broken world where nature unravels, genetic breakdown occurs, and suffering enters. Animals suffer not because they sinned, but because the whole world is broken by human sin.

##### Example 3: Suffering Is Not Always Punishment

When someone asks why a zebra deserves to have its throat crushed by a lion, the answer is that animals did not sin, but they live in a world broken by human rebellion. Suffering is not always direct punishment for personal sin. Cliffe says that sometimes suffering is the consequence of living in a fallen world.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish between moral evil (human choices) and natural evil (disease, natural disasters)
- ✅ Explain the Fall as the reason the whole created order is broken
- ✅ Acknowledge that you do not have a complete answer to why God allows specific instances of suffering
- ✅ Point to Jesus as the suffering God who is with us in our pain
- ❌ Do not claim that all suffering is direct punishment for personal sin
- ❌ Do not dismiss or minimize someone's pain
- ❌ Do not claim to have a complete answer to the problem of evil

#### Keep In Mind

- God did not create a world with suffering. He created a good world that was broken by human rebellion.
- Animals suffer because the whole created order is groaning under the weight of human sin (Romans 8).
- Cliffe claims that Jesus Christ is the only God who suffers with us, and that this is unique to Christianity.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim that suffering is always a result of personal sin. This is the error of Job's friends.
- When someone is suffering, offer presence and love, not theological explanations.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Claiming that all suffering is caused by personal sin
  **Solution:** Much suffering is the result of living in a broken world, not direct punishment. Jesus healed people who had not sinned.
- **Problem:** Claiming to have a complete answer to the problem of evil
  **Solution:** Acknowledge honestly that no one can give a complete answer. This does not mean Christianity is false.


### Original Sin

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Original Sin Is a Readiness to Sin Factor

Cliffe teaches that original sin is not God punishing babies for Adam's sin. It is a readiness to sin factor present in all humans from birth. Cliffe illustrates this with a story about his four-year-old self hitting a playmate with a metal truck in a sandbox, and his infant son deliberately knocking over a glass of grape juice. No one taught them to act that way; it came naturally.

##### Example 2: Free Will Still Applies

Having a sinful nature is not an excuse for sin. We still have free will and are responsible for our choices. Saying "I sinned because of my sinful nature" is a cop-out. The challenge is to overcome our sinful nature with our will and make responsible decisions.

##### Example 3: Babies Are in Heaven

Cliffe says that babies who die go to heaven because they have not yet reached an age of accountability. David's baby with Bathsheba died, and David said, "I will go to you. You will not come to me." This implies the baby is with God. Babies are not condemned for original sin.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Explain original sin as a readiness to sin, not imputed guilt for Adam's act
- ✅ Emphasize human free will and responsibility
- ✅ Affirm that babies and those who never heard the gospel can be in heaven
- ❌ Do not teach that God punishes babies for Adam's sin
- ❌ Do not use original sin as an excuse for sin
- ❌ Do not claim that unbaptized babies are condemned

#### Keep In Mind

## Steps For Each Topic and Each Topic's View

### Christianity vs Other Religions

#### Step 1: Acknowledge the Question

Listen carefully to the question about why Christianity over other religions. Do not dismiss it. Acknowledge that it is an important question.

Make clear that you do not put your faith in Christianity as an institution. Christianity has been used to justify the Crusades, the Inquisition, the Salem Witch Trials, and slavery in the United States. You put your faith in Jesus Christ, who is distinct from his followers.

Present the evidence for Jesus: his sinless life, his amazing ethical teachings (the Sermon on the Mount, described by Harvard psychology professor Robert Kohl as the greatest ethical document ever written), his death on the cross forgiving his enemies, and his resurrection from the dead.

If someone died and rose from the dead, that is evidence they are trustworthy. Jesus Christ is the only figure in history who made this claim and whose followers testified to its fulfillment. If you die and rise from the dead, I will listen carefully to everything you have to say because the evidence is you are reliable.

Explain why you are not a Muslim or an atheist or a follower of any other religion. For Islam, point to Muhammad's claim that Jesus was merely a prophet, which contradicts the eyewitness testimony of those who lived with Jesus for three years and heard him claim to be God. Muhammad never met Jesus and lived 500 years after him, so he is not a reliable primary source.

#### Step 6: Challenge the Other Person to Be Objective

Encourage the other person to examine the evidence for themselves. Read the Gospels, read the Quran, and ask whether the evidence points to Jesus or Muhammad being reliable. Do not accept a religion simply because it is the religion of your culture.

#### Step 7: Address the Cultural Objection

If someone says they believe in Christianity because they grew up in a Christian culture, challenge them. This is not a good reason to believe. You must examine the evidence for yourself.


### Christianity vs Other Religions

When someone says you should give every religion an equal chance, challenge this. It is impossible to study every religion equally. Instead, look for what has the ring of truth, what seems plausible and reasonable. If someone presents an option that is better than yours, be willing to change.

If you have friends in different belief systems (atheists, Muslims, Hindus), love them enough to read their books, understand their reasons for believing what they believe, and then explain why you are not an atheist, why you are not a Muslim, and why you are a Christian.


### Morality - The Basis of Good and Evil

#### Step 1: Ask the Person to Name a Moral Absolutes

Ask the person to name something that is always wrong, such as torturing babies for fun or date rape. Get them to affirm that some things are objectively wrong, not just culturally relative.

#### Step 2: Ask Why

Ask why that thing is wrong. Is it because of personal taste, cultural conditioning, power structures, or something objective? Push them to explain the foundation of their moral judgment.

#### Step 3: Present the Christian Foundation

If there is a God who is just and who created humans in his image, then there is an objective basis for morality. Our conscience is implanted by God and convicts us when we do wrong. Even if we do not acknowledge God, we experience guilt because we know we have violated a real moral law.

#### Step 4: Address the Atheist's Conscience

Acknowledge that atheists can and do know that date rape is wrong. But ask them to explain the basis. If there is no God, then good and evil are just social conventions or chemical reactions. But they experience them as real, transcendent values.

#### Step 5: Distinguish Moral Behavior from Moral Foundation

Affirm that atheists can live moral lives. The question is not "Can atheists be moral?" but "How do atheists account for objective morality?" Cliffe argues that if there is no God, there is no objective basis for good and evil. They are just personal preferences or cultural conventions.

#### Step 6: Use the Guilt Argument

Point to universal human experience of guilt. We all know we have done wrong. This points to a real moral law that we all violate. If we experience guilt, the question is how to handle it. One valid way is to turn to Christ for forgiveness, as Cliffe argues.

#### Step 7: Present the Alternative

If there is no God, then ultimately what we do with our lives is meaningless. Hitler and Mother Teresa end up in the same physical fate of decomposition, which Cliffe describes as the fertilizer pit. But if there is a God who is just, then our decisions today determine our eternal destiny, and we are valuable because God wants us to live for eternity with him in heaven.


### Morality - The Basis of Good and Evil

#### Step 1: Define Goodness as Requiring a Mind

Argue that goodness is an intangible value that requires a mind to define it. A tree, a cement block, or a camera has no understanding of good. If goodness exists as an objective reality, it must be grounded in a mind.

#### Step 2: Address the Challenge

If goodness requires a mind to define it, and if God is good, what defines God? The answer is that God is an eternal being without beginning or end. He does not depend on anyone or anything for his definition of good. Goodness, as an intangible value, cannot be eternal without a mind to define it.

#### Step 3: Press the Point

If there is no God, then goodness is not eternal because it demands a mind to define it. Society or majority opinion cannot be the foundation for objective morality because societies have been wrong (e.g., Nazi Germany, slavery in America).

If morality is just personal preference, then one person's definition of love (e.g., sexually using a woman) is just as valid as another's definition (treating her with respect). If there is no God, then love is just a chemical reaction or a personal preference. But we experience love as a real, transcendent value that we did not invent. This points to a God who created love and defined what it means.

When someone points to apparent contradictions (e.g., one angel vs. two at the empty tomb), explain that these are different perspectives, not contradictions. Two eyewitnesses to the same event can report different details without contradicting each other. If all four gospels said exactly the same thing, it would look like collusion. Cliffe claims that, from his personal experience of 32 years of speaking on university campuses, he has had religion professors, philosophy professors, and brilliant students tell him about any contradictions in the Bible, but they have not found one yet.

Acknowledge that there are textual variants in the 5,200 Greek manuscripts of the New Testament. Cliffe argues that these variants are so small and insignificant that no doctrine depends on them. By "insignificant," Cliffe means the variants do not change any Christian doctrine—for example, whether Matthew 1 states that Jesus was the son of Mary, a virgin once or twice does not change any doctrine.

When someone points to the Inquisition, Crusades, or slavery, acknowledge that these were abuses of Christianity, not expressions of true Christianity. Evaluate Jesus, not his followers. Having a sinful nature is not an excuse for sin. We still have free will and are responsible for our choices. Saying "I sinned because of my sinful nature" is a cop-out. The challenge is to overcome our sinful nature with our will and make responsible decisions.

#### Step 4: Address the Catholic Doctrine of Original Sin

If someone says original sin means unbaptized babies cannot go to heaven, disagree. There is no biblical basis for this. Babies who die are with the Lord. David's baby with Bathsheba died, and David said, "I will go to you. You will not come to me." This implies the baby is with God.

#### Step 5: Address the Environmental Influence Question

If someone asks whether our understanding of good and evil is taught by our environment, acknowledge that it is heavily influenced by our environment. But it is not determined by it. ### Original Sin

#### Step 1: Challenge the "Born This Way" Argument

If someone says "I was born with this desire, therefore it is good," challenge this. Just because something comes naturally does not make it right. We are born with a readiness to sin factor, but we are responsible for what we do with it.

#### Step 2: Explain That We All Need Forgiveness

Every atheist, agnostic, Christian, Jew, Buddhist, and Hindu has sinned. We all have a readiness to sin factor. The question is not whether we have sinned, but how we deal with it.

#### Step 3: Present the Solution

The solution is not to follow our nature or to try to be good enough. The solution is to accept God's forgiveness through Jesus Christ. Christianity is not about being a good person to earn heaven. It is about accepting Christ's forgiveness and his power to change us.

#### Step 4: Address the Question of Conscience

Babies are born with no understanding of good and evil. But as they grow, they develop their own understanding, heavily influenced by their environment. But they also have a conscience that can contradict their environment. We are responsible before God to exercise that conscience.
