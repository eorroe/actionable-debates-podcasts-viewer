# @LecraeOfficial on Faith, Pressure, and the Cost of Staying Honest ｜ The Unbound Podcast

## Overview

This podcast features an in-depth conversation with Lecrae about his personal journey through a crisis of faith, depression, and the pressure he faced while navigating racial tensions and political pressure within Christian communities. The discussion explores how Lecrae has maintained his integrity as a Christian hip-hop artist, the importance of contextualizing truth for different audiences, and the cost of being honest about one's struggles. The conversation also touches on legacy, generational bridges in ministry, and how God can reconstruct what the world has discarded.

## When to Follow Podcast

- When you need encouragement through a season of doubt, depression, or crisis of faith
- When working as a creative or artist trying to integrate faith with your craft
- When you feel pressure to conform your beliefs to fit into a cultural or political box
- When navigating racial tensions or political pressure within Christian communities
- When seeking to understand how to be a bridge between faith and contemporary culture

## Discussed

### Topic 1: Navigating Crisis of Faith and Depression

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Acknowledge the Reality of Your Struggle

When depression, anxiety, or doubt hit, do not dismiss them as something you can simply "get over." Lecrae initially thought these struggles were something you could just "get over yourself" about until he experienced them firsthand. The first step is to stop pretending and start being honest about what you are going through.

##### Step 2: Go Through the Pain Instead of Around It

Lecrae emphasizes that the way to get over these difficulties is to go through them. Avoidance or rushing past pain does not lead to healing. Real recovery requires sitting with the discomfort and depending on God in the middle of it.

##### Step 3: Reject the Temptation to Put God in a Box

During his dark season, Lecrae realized he had confined God to a limited version of Christianity based on what he saw in American churches. You cannot take your limited worldview and force God into that box while still calling him God. Allow your understanding of God to expand beyond cultural or political boundaries.

##### Step 4: Seek Global Perspective

Lecrae's turning point came during a trip to Egypt where he encountered a non-Christian archaeologist who acknowledged biblical history without knowing the Bible. This reminded him that the Christian faith is global, not American. Expose yourself to perspectives beyond your immediate circle to see the bigger picture of God's kingdom.

##### Step 5: Choose the Kingdom Over the Empire

When you feel disillusioned with the church, culture, or systems, ask yourself what is bigger: the kingdom or the empire? Lecrae had to decide whether to abandon his Christian faith because of broken American Christianity or to hold onto the global kingdom of God.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Lecrae's Crisis of Faith

After Trayvon Martin was killed around 2012–2013, Lecrae expressed grief on social media and faced pushback from believers who told him to stop being political. This launched him into a dark season where he became disillusioned with the church's racial and political divisions. He wrote lyrics describing being "mad at Christians" and thinking "the church was disgusting" before the Holy Spirit patiently stirred his faith back to life.

##### Example 2: The Egypt Trip Turning Point

While visiting tombs in Egypt with his wife, Lecrae's non-Christian tour guide acknowledged a Pharaoh who freed the slaves without knowing the Bible. Lecrae realized the Christian story is real and global. This encounter pulled him back from abandoning his faith entirely and showed him that God is bigger than American politics or church culture.

##### Example 3: The Reconstructed Tour and Album

Lecrae's "Reconstruction" album and world tour are built on the metaphor of Michelangelo's David, which was sculpted from discarded marble. This represents God's work of taking people and things the world has thrown away and rebuilding them. The tour takes this message to Zimbabwe, Zambia, Kenya, South Africa, Australia, New Zealand, and the United States, celebrating reconstruction while also mourning those still in need of healing.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Be honest about your mental health struggles rather than pretending they do not exist
- ✅ Allow yourself to grieve losses, including disillusionment with the church or faith community
- ✅ Seek perspectives outside your immediate cultural and national bubble
- ✅ Hold tightly to the core of your faith while being willing to release cultural trappings
- ✅ Find mentors and elders who can speak into your life during confusion
- ✅ Use your pain as a bridge to help others who are struggling
- ✅ Remember that God uses broken things to reveal his glory

#### Keep In Mind

- Lecrae's experience shows that depression and anxiety are real struggles
- Lecrae's pain did not disqualify him from ministry or purpose
- Lecrae was disillusioned with church culture but did not abandon his faith
- God met Lecrae in his dark season and used it for good
- Lecrae's testimony of survival can become someone else's lifeline

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Be cautious about the platforms and audiences you share deep personal pain with
- Seek professional counseling or pastoral support alongside community sharing
- Do not isolate yourself during a crisis of faith; maintain connection to safe people
- Guard your heart against those who would dismiss your pain as simply a lack of faith

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to convince everyone to see your perspective during a cultural or racial crisis, which drains your energy
  **Solution:** Focus on being faithful to your convictions rather than winning every argument. Let your life and work speak for you.
- **Problem:** Throwing out your entire faith because of hurtful actions by Christians or church institutions
  **Solution:** Separate the message of Jesus from the failures of his followers. Recognize that the church is made up of broken people, and God's kingdom transcends any single institution.
- **Problem:** Confining God to your cultural, political, or denominational box
  **Solution:** Regularly expose yourself to the global, historical church. Read widely, travel when possible, and listen to voices from different backgrounds.
- **Problem:** Moving so fast in ministry or purpose that you neglect your own healing and growth
  **Solution:** Slow down. There is urgency in the message, but there is no need to rush your personal formation. Listen to those who have walked the path before you.

### Topic 2: Contextualizing Faith Through Art and Culture

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Identify Your Unique Brokenness as a Ministry Tool

Lecrae grew up in a broken home and was raised in hip-hop culture, which he describes as a "broken thing." Recognize the specific brokenness or cultural context of your own life, because God uses those exact experiences to bring healing to others.

##### Step 2: Learn the Culture You Want to Reach

Before you can contextualize truth for a specific audience, you must understand that culture deeply. Lecrae immersed himself in hip-hop history, understanding its roots in the Bronx, its evolution, and its disenfranchised communities. Know the language, values, and pain points of the people you want to reach.

##### Step 3: Translate Biblical Truth Without Compromising It

Contextualization means making the truth accessible, not changing the truth. Lecrae uses analogies, stories, and the language of hip-hop to communicate biblical principles without diluting their meaning. Find the bridge between timeless truth and contemporary expression.

##### Step 4: Expect Resistance and Prepare Your Response

Early in his career, Lecrae faced pushback from Christians who questioned whether hip-hop could be used for God. They said hip-hop only talks about killing and drug dealing. Lecrae responded by pointing out that a lot of contemporary gospel songs came from the blues, which was also once considered a degenerate art form. Prepare thoughtful responses to critics by studying history and finding biblical precedents.

##### Step 5: Use Your Platform to Create Bridges

Lecrae describes himself as standing in the gap between the hip-hop world and the church, translating languages for both sides. Your unique position can serve as a bridge if you intentionally build connections between communities that otherwise would not understand each other.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Lecrae's Analogy of Young David and King Saul

When critics questioned Lecrae's unconventional approach, he used the story of young David approaching King Saul and saying, "This armor doesn't quite fit me. But I promise if you let me wear what I have and give me my sling, I'll get the job done." This illustrates how God uses people in their own authentic style rather than forcing them into predetermined boxes.

##### Example 2: Song "Take Me as I Am"

Lecrae wrote this song after waking up at 5:46 a.m. with Ephesians on his mind, articulating that he thought he had to earn God's love but realized God would take him as he was. Countless people responded with "That's me. That's me," indicating they also felt they had to earn God's love. This demonstrates how personal testimony expressed authentically can lead others to Christ.

##### Example 3: Generational Bridge in Ministry

Lecrae's pastor in Atlanta, Pastor Lee Jenkins in his mid-60s, creates an environment where older and younger generations feel valued. He brings young people up to speak and finds ways to bridge generational gaps. Lecrae emphasizes that churches that fail to pass the baton between generations eventually decline, as illustrated by a congregation that started with 100 members and has since shrunk to 85 with no new members joining.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Treat your unique background and brokenness as preparation for ministry, not shame
- ✅ Study the history and culture of the art form or community you want to reach
- ✅ Find analogies and stories that make biblical truth accessible without compromising it
- ✅ Learn from both older mentors and younger innovators to stay balanced
- ✅ Be willing to look different or be misunderstood in order to reach new people
- ✅ Remember that contemporary gospel art forms often have roots in previously marginalized styles
- ✅ Build bridges rather than walls between communities of faith

#### Keep In Mind

- Hip-hop was born in the 1970s in the Bronx from disenfranchised youth, not as a spiritual movement but as a cultural one
- All art forms mature, and the question is not whether a medium is inherently holy, but how it is used
- Being "sanctified" or "Christianized" does not mean adopting the culture's values; it means redeeming the medium for kingdom purposes
- Resistance to new expressions of faith is common
- The goal is to make the gospel accessible to people where they are

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When sharing your faith through art, ensure your message aligns with Scripture rather than simply appealing to audience preferences
- Be cautious about adopting cultural values that contradict biblical principles in an effort to be relevant
- Maintain accountability to mature believers who can help you stay grounded in truth
- Do not allow your platform to become your identity; your identity is found in Christ
- Be aware that reaching both the church and the culture simultaneously can create tension; prepare for criticism from both sides

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Becoming so focused on convincing critics that you lose sight of your actual calling
  **Solution:** Keep your eyes on the people you are reaching rather than the people you are fighting. Let your life and work speak for you.
- **Problem:** Letting cultural acceptance pressure you to compromise biblical truth
  **Solution:** The means of communication can adapt, but the message must remain anchored in Scripture. Relevance never requires sacrificing truth.
- **Problem:** Becoming dependent on being "cool" or "hip" as a church or ministry leader
  **Solution:** If all you offer are "hit records" or trendy programs, you will eventually have nothing new to offer. Invest in the wisdom of elders and the long-term formation of your community.
- **Problem:** Forgetting the broken and disenfranchised origins of the culture you are engaging
  **Solution:** Regularly revisit why you started and who you are trying to reach. Do not let success or institutional acceptance make you forget the margins.

### Topic 3: Legacy, Authenticity, and Being a Bridge Between Faith and Culture

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Examine the Pride That Comes With Knowledge

Lecrae candidly admits that in his younger years, his biblical and theological knowledge made him arrogant. He thought he was better than others because he knew more. A critical step in growth is recognizing when knowledge has become a source of pride rather than humility, and actively pursuing empathy and humility instead.

##### Step 2: Slow Down and Listen to Older, Wiser Voices

Lecrae wishes he had slowed down in his younger years. He was in a rush to grow up, fast-forward through seasons, and get to the next place. He advises his younger self to listen to those who have walked the path before, especially older believers who can offer wisdom about navigating life stages he has not yet experienced, such as parenting teenagers.

##### Step 3: Define Legacy by What You Leave in People, Not What You Achieve

Lecrae was challenged by Dr. Crawford Loritts' statement: "Legacy is what you leave in people." While awards and accomplishments are visible, the true measure of a life is the spiritual and moral formation of those you have influenced. Focus on pouring into people rather than building a personal brand.

##### Step 4: Be a Bridge Between Worlds

Lecrae's greatest desire is to stand in the gap between the hip-hop world and the church, translating languages for both sides. He wants people to see that he was deeply committed to practicing the way of Jesus while also being culturally relevant. Being a bridge requires fluency in multiple worlds and a willingness to be misunderstood by both.

##### Step 5: Embrace the Reconstruction Mindset

The reconstruction theme of Lecrae's album and tour is rooted in the idea that humans and communities can be discarded by the world, but God wants to rebuild them. Adopt this mindset for your own life and ministry: recognize that brokenness is not the end, and God specializes in taking discarded pieces and making something beautiful.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Lecrae's Reflection on Younger Self vs. Older Self

When asked what he would tell his younger self, Lecrae names specific struggles: youthful arrogance based on biblical knowledge, a puffed-up attitude toward those less theologically literate, and a rush to fast-forward through life. He would tell younger him to learn empathy, humility, and patience. This raw self-assessment models the kind of honesty he advocates.

##### Example 2: The Generational Wisdom Gap

Lecrae, now in his 40s with teenagers, recognizes he needs older mentors because he has never parented a teenager before, and his teenagers have never been teenagers before. He appreciates Pastor Lee Jenkins' model of valuing both older and younger generations rather than discarding one for the other. This demonstrates the practical necessity of intergenerational relationships.

##### Example 3: Lecrae's 20-Year Marriage and Family

Lecrae and his wife will celebrate 20 years of marriage. He met her at the University of North Texas when she was driving from Denton to Oak Cliff Bible Fellowship on Sundays. Their story is grounded in church community and long-term commitment, providing an example of long-term commitment.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Regularly examine your motives and pride, especially in spiritual knowledge or ministry success
- ✅ Seek out mentors from older generations who can speak wisdom into your current season
- ✅ Define success by spiritual impact on people rather than awards, sales, or recognition
- ✅ Be willing to be misunderstood by both the church and the culture as you bridge gaps
- ✅ Practice patience with yourself and others; growth takes time
- ✅ Invest in long-term relationships rather than temporary popularity
- ✅ Keep your story of brokenness and redemption central to your identity

#### Keep In Mind

- Pride disguised as spiritual maturity is one of the destructive temptations
- Zeal and hunger for God are gifts, but there is no need to rush your personal formation
- Legacy is not what you leave to people in terms of inheritance, but what you leave in people
- Being a bridge means being stretched between two worlds; it is Kingdom work
- The gospel is always relevant

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When navigating multiple cultural worlds, be honest about which parts of each culture align with biblical truth and which do not
- Do not compromise your convictions to fit into either the church or the culture; integrity requires holding steady
- Be careful not to use your platform to air grievances or settle scores, even when you have been hurt
- Maintain accountability structures in both the faith community and the creative community
- Guard your heart against bitterness when you are misunderstood or rejected by either side

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Letting knowledge puff you up, causing you to look down on others who are less theologically literate
  **Solution:** Regularly remind yourself that knowledge can lead to pride, as Lecrae experienced in his younger years. Pursue humility as actively as you pursue learning.
- **Problem:** Rushing through life seasons and trying to fast-forward to the next milestone
  **Solution:** Recognize that each season has purpose. Slow down and allow God to form you in the waiting.
- **Problem:** Defining your legacy by external achievements rather than the spiritual health of those around you
  **Solution:** Listen to trusted people who can speak into your life about your impact on others.
- **Problem:** Burning out by trying to please both the church and the culture simultaneously
  **Solution:** Remember that your primary audience is God. Stay faithful to him, and he will give you the grace to navigate the tensions.
