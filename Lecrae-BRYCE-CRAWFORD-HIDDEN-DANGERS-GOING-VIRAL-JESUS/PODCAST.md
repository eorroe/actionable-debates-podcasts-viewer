# Bryce Crawford Exposes The Hidden Dangers of Going Viral for Jesus

## Overview

Bryce Crawford, a 21-year-old Christian evangelist and content creator, shares his journey from crippling depression and a near-suicide attempt on Christmas Day 2020 to becoming a bold voice for Jesus on social media and in public spaces. He discusses the hidden dangers of treating Christianity as merely a "vibe" rather than a lifestyle, the challenges of digital influence, and practical approaches to mental health that balance supernatural healing with therapy. Through personal stories from Burning Man, street evangelism, and online ministry, Bryce and Lecrae explore what it means to live out authentic faith in Gen Z's hyper-connected world.

## When to Follow Podcast

- When you are a young Christian navigating digital influence and social media with integrity
- When you or someone you know struggles with mental health, depression, or anxiety as a believer
- When you want to learn how to share the gospel authentically with strangers, especially in hostile or unconventional environments

## Discussed

### Topic 1: Street Evangelism - How to share the gospel with strangers

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Ask God to send you to the darkest places

Pray and ask God to send you to the most hurt people, to the places where people are most broken, and to use you as the person who shares the gospel with them.

##### Step 2: Listen before you speak

When approaching someone, start by asking how they are doing and genuinely listening to their story. Do not lead with preaching or judgment. Let them feel seen and heard before you share anything about Jesus.

##### Step 3: Earn trust through genuine care

Build rapport by showing up consistently. Let people know you care about them as a person, not just as a target for evangelism. Follow up with people you meet in the street through text or future encounters.

##### Step 4: Share your story vulnerably

After establishing trust, ask if you can share a little bit of your story. Be honest about your own brokenness and how Jesus changed your life. Authenticity opens doors that argument never will.

##### Step 5: Do not argue or egg people on

If someone gets offended or upset, do not engage in an argument. Your goal is not to win a debate but to love the person. Pray for them even if they reject you.

##### Step 6: Play the long game

Understand that you may not see immediate fruit. Seeds you plant may take years to grow. Trust God with the timeline and keep showing up.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Waffle House encounter

On Christmas Day 2020, Bryce went to Waffle House planning to end his life. He reached over to a stranger who was alone and asked to share a table. The man began pouring out his own life issues—his wife divorcing him, losing his kids, feeling unloved. The man said, "There's no growth in a relationship if the love isn't mutual." That moment stopped Bryce in his tracks and opened his heart to the possibility that God loved him. He ran out crying, went to his car, and asked Jesus to take away his anxiety and depression. That was the moment he became a Christian.

##### Example 2: Spit on at a club

Lecrae shared a story about being spit on while sharing the gospel in line at a club. He felt embarrassed and angry but chose to pray for the man instead of retaliating. About three years later, a guy came up to him at an event and said he became a Christian that night in line because he saw Lecrae respond with grace instead of anger.

##### Example 3: Burning Man encounters

Bryce shared stories of being invited to an orgy bus and being offered LSD at Burning Man. He declined and used those moments as opportunities to share the gospel, saying he was "all about Jesus." He acknowledged there were many videos he could not even post because of how wild the environment was.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do listen more than you talk
- ✅ Do ask people about their lives before sharing yours
- ✅ Do follow up with people you meet
- ✅ Do pray for people who reject or mock you
- ✅ Don't argue or try to win debates
- ✅ Don't treat people as projects or targets
- ✅ Don't get prideful because you are doing what others are afraid to do

#### Keep In Mind

- The highest rate of conversion is generally through relationships, not street evangelism. That is why following up matters.
- People remember how you made them feel, not what you said.
- Persecution is not the same as someone getting riled up because you started an argument.
- You may be the only Christian that person ever encounters. Represent Jesus well.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Always be aware of your surroundings when evangelizing in public or unfamiliar places.
- If a situation becomes physically threatening, remove yourself and pray for the person from a safe distance.
- Do not film people without their consent, especially in vulnerable moments.
- When meeting strangers, consider letting someone know where you are.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Evangelizing out of spiritual pride, thinking you are better than the person you are talking to.
  **Solution:** Remember that you were once broken too. See people as equals in need of grace, not as projects to fix.
- **Problem:** Getting discouraged when no one responds or when you face hostility.
  **Solution:** Trust the long game. Seeds take time to grow. Focus on obedience, not results.
- **Problem:** Arguing with people instead of loving them.
  **Solution:** Your goal is to point people to Jesus, not to win an argument. If they get angry, pray for them and walk away.
- **Problem:** Treating evangelism as a numbers game.
  **Solution:** Quality relationships matter more than quantity. One genuine conversation is better than 100 shallow ones.

### Topic 2: Mental Health & Depression - Balancing faith with practical mental health care

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Acknowledge the struggle

Admit to yourself and to God that you are dealing with anxiety or depression. Do not feel shame for it. Mental health struggles are real and affect many believers.

##### Step 2: Seek practical help alongside prayer

Do not rely solely on prayer. Use practical tools like therapy, medication prescribed by doctors, and accountability relationships. God gives wisdom to medical professionals just as he gives wisdom to pastors.

##### Step 3: Build a support system

Have people in your life who can be real with you. Create a group chat or circle of friends where you can confess when you are making dumb decisions before you make them.

##### Step 4: Practice rhythms of rest

Find rhythms of rest that work for you. This could mean Sabbath, hobbies, sleep, or simply disconnecting. A quiet life protects your mental health.

##### Step 5: Be honest about your ongoing battles

Even after supernatural healing, you may still experience occasional struggles. Acknowledge them openly so others do not think you have it all together.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Christmas Day near-suicide

At 17, Bryce battled crippling anxiety and depression and planned to end his life on Christmas Day 2020. He went to Waffle House for a last meal, where a stranger's words about love being mutual unexpectedly pierced his heart. For the first time, his head knowledge about God connected with his heart. He asked Jesus to take away his anxiety and depression and felt immediate peace. He has not experienced crippling anxiety since that day, though he did have an anxiety attack two weeks before this podcast.

##### Example 2: Therapy as a tool

Bryce went to therapy from ages 18 through 20 and praised God for the wisdom his therapist Juliet gave him. He emphasized that therapy was a practical tool that helped him learn about himself and be dependent on God.

##### Example 3: Lecrae's mental health journey

Lecrae shared that he probably had his first mental breakdown at 22 and went to a mental hospital for a few days. He noted that social media now statistically contributes to 39% of people's mental health deterioration.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do be honest about your mental health struggles
- ✅ Do combine faith with practical tools like therapy and medication
- ✅ Do have a support system of mentors and friends
- ✅ Do practice rest and Sabbath
- ❌ Don't feel shame for struggling with mental health
- ❌ Don't rely only on prayer and ignore practical help
- ❌ Don't pretend you have it all together when you don't

#### Keep In Mind

- God can heal supernaturally, but he also gives wisdom to doctors and therapists.
- Anxiety and depression make you feel shameful for dealing with it, but you are not alone.
- Social media can contribute to mental health deterioration. Be mindful of your consumption.
- Even after healing, occasional struggles can still happen. That is normal.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- If you are having thoughts of suicide, reach out immediately to a crisis helpline or trusted person.
- Be careful about oversharing mental health details on social media where they can be used against you.
- Choose a therapist who shares your values or at least respects your faith.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that needing therapy or medication means you lack faith.
  **Solution:** God uses doctors and medicine. Practical tools are not a lack of trust in God.
- **Problem:** Isolating yourself because you feel ashamed of your struggles.
  **Solution:** Isolation feeds depression. Reach out to trusted people who can walk with you.
- **Problem:** Expecting instant healing and giving up when it does not happen immediately.
  **Solution:** Healing is often a process. Stay in community, use tools, and keep trusting God.
- **Problem:** Using social media as a primary source of comfort or community.
  **Solution:** Social media contributes to mental health deterioration. Invest in real-life relationships.

### Topic 3: Digital Influence & Social Media - Being a Christian creator online with integrity

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Examine your heart posture

Before you post anything, ask yourself: am I doing this to be seen by others, or am I doing it because God led me to? If your goal is to be seen by people, you already have your reward.

##### Step 2: Keep a private life

Maintain hobbies, rhythms, and relationships that are just for you. You do not need to share everything with the internet. Having a private life protects your heart and keeps you grounded.

##### Step 3: Be honest about your imperfections

Do not pretend to be perfect. Admit when you make mistakes. People can relate to brokenness, not perfection. Have a few trusted mentors who can be real with you and call you out.

##### Step 4: Prioritize local church and discipleship

Do not let online ministry replace your local church and discipleship relationships. The Christian content industry often fails when creators focus on views instead of discipleship.

##### Step 5: Use media as a tool, not an identity

Social media is a platform, not your life. Do not become a slave to posting, algorithm changes, or internet fame. Live open-handed, willing to walk away if God calls you to.

##### Step 6: Vet what you consume

Be intentional about what you watch and listen to. What you consume shapes who you become. Cut off content that produces gossip, drama, or sin in your heart.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Love Island conviction

Bryce watched Love Island and got caught up in the TikTok swirl. He found himself gossiping about people he had never met. He realized he was becoming a product of the show—gossip-filled and addicted to drama. He cut it out, saying, "That's the last thing I want to do."

##### Example 2: Dark side of Christian content creation

Bryce warned that the dark side of Christian content creation is the pressure to produce content just because you are saved. He said half of the Christian content creators he knew when he first became a Christian are no longer Christians. Christianity has become a label, not a lifestyle.

##### Example 3: Keeping a quiet life

Bryce emphasized that he loves having a private life. He enjoys basketball, fishing, and the beach—things that are just for him and his friends. He only posts what he feels the Lord leads him to post. If the Lord does not lead him to share something, he keeps it to himself.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do examine your heart before posting
- ✅ Do keep a private life separate from your online presence
- ✅ Do be honest about your failures and imperfections
- ✅ Do prioritize local church and discipleship over content creation
- ✅ Do vet your media consumption carefully
- ❌ Don't post for the sake of views or popularity
- ❌ Don't let online ministry replace real-life community
- ❌ Don't pretend to have it all together

#### Keep In Mind

- Very few people have a voice in your life. Many have opinions, but only a few have earned the right to speak into your life.
- If your whole goal is to be seen by others, you already have your reward.
- Christianity becoming popular is both a blessing and a danger. Many people are genuinely coming to Jesus, but many are also treating faith as a brand.
- You do not have to advertise your music or your faith. Make good content, and if people hear it and their heart changes, that is enough.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share location data or personal routines publicly.
- Be cautious about filming vulnerable people or sensitive situations without consent.
- Keep your inner circle small and trustworthy. Do not let internet comments shape your identity.
- Remember that mistakes are amplified when you have a large following. Have accountability in place before you need it.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Posting content to be seen by others rather than to honor God.
  **Solution:** Ask God before you post. If he does not lead you to share it, keep it private.
- **Problem:** Becoming a slave to the algorithm or internet fame.
  **Solution:** Live open-handed. Be willing to disappear from the internet with a flip phone if God calls you to.
- **Problem:** Letting online ministry replace local church and discipleship.
  **Solution:** Your online platform should point people to your local church and to Jesus, not replace them.
- **Problem:** Thinking Christianity is just a vibe or a label.
  **Solution:** Following Jesus is denying yourself, picking up your cross, and going all in. It is not just attending Bible studies or posting verses.

### Topic 4: Gen Z Spirituality - Engaging young people with authentic Christianity

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Read your Bible for yourself

Do not rely solely on social media snippets or influencers for your spiritual understanding. Read the Bible yourself. Biblical literacy is a lost art, and Gen Z is biblically illiterate.

##### Step 2: Take practical action steps

Faith requires action. If you struggle with sin, take practical steps to remove temptation. Do not just pray for God to fix everything while you keep your phone in your hand at midnight.

##### Step 3: Seek wisdom from older, mature believers

Find mentors who are older and wiser than you. Learn from married couples, pastors, and people who have walked the path before you. You cannot be in authority unless you have been under authority.

##### Step 4: Do not settle in relationships

For young men: lead well, do not play with people's hearts. For young women: do not settle for less than a godly man. Know people by their fruits. Ask God for permission before pursuing relationships.

##### Step 5: Engage with culture without being consumed by it

Gen Z is spiritually curious but also biblically illiterate. Meet people where they are, whether that is at Burning Man, on the street, or online. But do not let culture shape your identity more than Christ shapes it.

##### Step 6: Believe the best in people

When you see someone sharing their faith journey online, assume it is genuine until proven otherwise. Love hopes the best in people. Do not be quick to call something fake.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Biblical illiteracy

Bryce mentioned seeing a video where a guy asked people at a school what the Bible verse in their Instagram bio meant, and they could not even quote the verse they had posted. He emphasized reading the Bible for yourself and not relying only on influencers.

##### Example 2: The Jesus glow trend

Lecrae asked Bryce about the "Jesus glow" trend where people show their before and after lives. Bryce said it encourages him because it shows radical change from Jesus. He acknowledged some people might do it performatively, but either way, it shows that God can change lives.

##### Example 3: Christianity as a vibe

Bryce observed that Christianity has become popular with Gen Z, but the danger is that it becomes a label, not a lifestyle. He said the pro is many are genuinely turning to Jesus, but the con is that many are just following a trend.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do read your Bible for yourself
- ✅ Do take practical action steps alongside prayer
- ✅ Do seek mentors and accountability
- ✅ Do lead well in relationships
- ✅ Do believe the best in people
- ❌ Don't rely only on social media for spiritual content
- ❌ Don't treat Christianity as a vibe or label
- ❌ Don't settle for less in relationships
- ❌ Don't be quick to judge others' authenticity

#### Keep In Mind

- Gen Z is more spiritually curious than previous generations, but they are also the most biblically illiterate.
- Christianity becoming popular is both an opportunity and a danger.
- Time does not automatically bring maturity. Supernatural maturity happens when you are actively walking with God and applying what you learn.
- Wisdom is not just knowledge. It is applying what you learn and getting actual experience.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Be careful about what you share publicly about your relationships or private life.
- When engaging with strangers online or in person, have accountability and boundaries.
- Do not put yourself in dangerous situations just to evangelize. Use wisdom.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being biblically illiterate and relying on social media for theology.
  **Solution:** Read your Bible for yourself. Use Bible college, books, and tools, but do not rely only on one influencer.
- **Problem:** Treating Christianity as a vibe or identity label rather than a lifestyle.
  **Solution:** Ask yourself daily if you are denying yourself, picking up your cross, and following Jesus. Going to Bible study is not the same as following Jesus.
- **Problem:** Entitlement in dating and relationships.
  **Solution:** Ask God for permission before pursuing someone. Lead well as a man. Do not settle as a woman. Learn from married mentors.
- **Problem:** Judging others' authenticity too quickly.
  **Solution:** Love hopes the best in people. Even if someone is performative, God can still use it to reach others. Focus on your own heart.
