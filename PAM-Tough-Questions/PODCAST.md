# Philip Anthony Mitchell Answers the Questions Everyone Is Asking

## Overview

This podcast provides clarity and context around the intense public scrutiny, security measures, slander, and criticism Philip Anthony Mitchell faces as a pastor. It explains why the Street Preachers podcast tour charges fees, addresses how Christians should respond to online slander, and shares how to remain faithful and content while under public pressure. Listeners gain practical insight into navigating persecution, leadership accountability, and faithful ministry in a hostile culture.

## When to Follow Podcast

- When you need to understand how to respond to public slander and criticism as a Christian leader
- When working through the tension between gospel accessibility and the practical costs of ministry operations
- When the user asks about maintaining personal holiness, contentment, and family stability amid intense public scrutiny

## Discussed

### Topic 1

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Respond to Slander with Prayer, Not Retaliation

When facing public slander or threats, train yourself and your family to pray for persecutors rather than retaliate on social media. Follow Jesus' teaching that if the world hated Him first, it will hate His followers. Refuse to slander back or fight in the same medium where you are attacked.

##### Step 2: Submit to Legal and Governing Authority for Safety

If legal counsel, board of directors, or attorneys require security measures for travel or public appearances, acquiesce to their guidance. View yourself as a steward, not an owner, of the ministry. Safety protocols including security personnel are not a status flex but a legal requirement when credible threats have been made.

##### Step 3: Accept Criticism as Part of Faithful Proclamation

Make a vow to God to preach whatever the Holy Spirit tells you, regardless of series length or topic. Navigate criticism on your knees and in tears. Accept that sharing in Christ's sufferings is part of faithful ministry, just as sharing in His blessings is.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Teaching Children to Forgive Persecutors

Philip instructs his children never to hate those who threaten or slander their father. Instead, they are to pray for those people because Jesus taught that persecution comes with the territory of following Him. This protects the family's heart while modeling Christ-like response.

##### Example 2: Navigating Tour Pricing Criticism

When people push back on ticket fees for the Street Preachers podcast tour, listen first rather than react with frustration. Explain that proceeds cover venues, worship leaders, travel, hotels, insurance, and production. Distinguish between fair theological critique (why charge for the gospel?) and outright hatred that will criticize regardless of what you do.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do pray for those who persecute you rather than retaliate or slander back
- ✅ Do submit to governing authorities, boards, and legal counsel for safety decisions
- ✅ Also do navigate criticism privately through prayer and tears rather than public defense
- ❌ Don't post on social media to fight back against slander
- ❌ Don't view security requirements as optional if legally mandated by ministry leadership

#### Keep In Mind

- The only way to completely avoid criticism is to do nothing
- There are two types of critics: those with fair theological questions and those who hate regardless of your actions
- If you preach the text faithfully, it will ruffle feathers and agitate camps

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Credible threats have included people showing up at offices and gathering locations, requiring security presence
- Weapons including knives and guns have been taken from individuals at events
- Someone attempted to approach Philip in the pulpit and grab him on multiple occasions
- Security is legally required when leaving Atlanta or being in certain spaces, as mandated by board of directors and attorneys

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Reacting with frustration or anger when people challenge ministry finances or pricing
**Solution:** Listen to the concern, understand the critique, and explain the operational costs without becoming defensive

**Problem:** Trying to publicly defend yourself against every slanderous comment on social media
**Solution:** Train your family and team to pray for persecutors and avoid social media retaliation entirely

### Topic 2

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Distinguish Between Fair Critique and Hateful Opposition

When receiving criticism, categorize it into those asking fair theological questions (e.g., "If it's the gospel, why charge?") versus those who will hate you no matter what. Respond to the former with clarity and grace; do not waste energy trying to convince the latter.

##### Step 2: Explain Operational Costs Transparently

For ministry events with fees, break down what tickets actually cover: venue rental, worship leaders, travel, hotels, insurance, and production. Clarify that proceeds are not for personal enrichment but to make the event possible.

##### Step 3: Maintain Free Access for Local Congregations

Continue offering free access to your local church with no offering required. Keep podcast and touring entities separate from local church operations so that local ministry remains accessible to everyone regardless of ability to pay.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Street Preachers Tour Pricing Model

The Street Preachers podcast operates as a separate entity from the local 2019 church, powered by disciples with their own resources. Ticket prices around $60 fund the entire touring operation. The church maintains free access, so both accessibility and sustainability are preserved.

##### Example 2: Pastor Charging for Ministry

When asked why a pastor charges for events, explain that without ticket fees, venues, worship leaders, travel, insurance, and production cannot happen. Transparency about operational reality addresses the fair critique without apologizing for basic stewardship.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do separate local church ministry from podcast and touring entities financially and operationally
- ✅ Do explain operational costs when questioned about fees
- ✅ Also do listen to criticism before responding, especially from those with genuine concerns
- ❌ Don't conflate local church free access with touring or podcast production costs
- ❌ Don't assume all criticism comes from hate; some comes from legitimate theological questions

#### Keep In Mind

- 2019 church offers access for free with no offering
- The Street Preachers podcast is a completely separate entity powered by disciples with resources
- Tim Timberlake's vision was to tour, which requires logistical and financial structures that local church operations do not

#### Security & Safety Notes

- None specific to this topic beyond general ministry travel safety already covered

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Becoming angry or defensive when people question why a pastor charges for events
**Solution:** Listen first, understand the concern, then explain the practical costs of touring without taking it personally

**Problem:** Allowing fair critiques and hateful comments to be handled with the same response
**Solution:** Give fair critiques clear, gracious answers; do not waste energy arguing with those determined to hate regardless

### Topic 3

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Model Forgiveness in Family and Ministry Culture

When you hear family members or ministry team members slandering one another, intervene with the same heartbreak you would feel if your own children were fighting. Use that emotional response to teach that demonizing others is wrong, especially within the body of Christ.

##### Step 2: Address Public Slander by Modeling Private Resolution

Teach that most conflict should be handled by praying for the person or speaking to them privately, not by posting publicly on social media. Public slander on social media is given a platform it does not deserve and causes real harm to unity and witness.

##### Step 3: Acknowledge the Reality of Self-Righteous Behavior in the Church

When examples of Christians attacking others publicly come to your attention, speak plainly about how this behavior is exactly why people stay away from church. The church is a beautiful place but a glorious mess, and self-righteous public attacks are part of that mess.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Children Fighting and the Heavenly Father's Grief

Philip used the example of hearing his four kids fight and demonize each other as a child to illustrate how the heavenly Father feels when His children attack one another on social media. If an imperfect father felt grieved, how much more does God grieve over public slander among believers?

##### Example 2: Attacking a Friend's Wedding Photos

A close friend journeying in faith posted wedding pictures, and Christians attacked his wife's dress in the comments. The friend asked, "Is this why people don't want to go to church?" Philip affirmed that self-righteous behavior in the body of Christ is exactly why people avoid the church.

##### Example 3: Storefront Preacher Perspective

A storefront preacher burdened to see Jesus preached thanked God for Philip because he cannot reach 80,000 people at AT&T Stadium. This frames criticism within the reality that God uses different ministers for different assignments, and slander often ignores the actual fruit of ministry.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do feel grief when believers slander one another and let that motivate reconciliation
- ✅ Do address public slander by calling people to private conversation or prayer instead
- ✅ Also do acknowledge openly when Christian behavior is driving people away from the church
- ❌ Don't use social media to demonize fellow believers even when you disagree with them
- ❌ Don't ignore slander in the body of Christ as "just the way things are"

#### Keep In Mind

- Social media has given everyone a platform for an opinion, but not everyone has a mandate to publicly slander
- There are times to call out wrong publicly, but most conflict should be handled privately or through prayer
- Even a storefront preacher with a smaller platform can see the value in larger ministry reach

#### Security & Safety Notes

- None specific to this topic

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Using social media as the primary forum to address conflict with other believers
**Solution:** Move conflict resolution to private conversation or prayer; reserve public calls for genuine doctrinal or ethical wrongdoing

**Problem:** Becoming calloused to slander within the church because it feels normal
**Solution:** Let the grief you feel when believers attack each other drive you to teach and model a better way

### Topic 4

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Establish Boundaries to Protect Family

When threats, slander, and online harassment become severe enough that family members must navigate hostile environments, set firm boundaries. Philip's wife no longer goes on social media, and children are taught how to handle spaces where their father's name comes up.

##### Step 2: Teach Children to Respond with Grace Under Pressure

Train your children to never hate those who persecute their father, to pray for them, and to understand that persecution comes with following Jesus. Equip them with a theological framework for why people react negatively so they are not destroyed by it.

##### Step 3: Balance Ministry Fruit with Family Safety

Recognize that families see both the danger and the fruit of ministry. Threats and slander are real, but so are lives being impacted and people being healed. Let both realities inform your decisions rather than allowing fear alone to drive every choice.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Wife Withdrawing from Social Media

Philip's wife no longer participates in social media because of the threats and slander directed at the family. This boundary protects her peace while Philip continues to engage publicly.

##### Example 2: Children Navigating School and Social Spaces

Philip's children still have to be in school and spaces where his name comes up. They must learn to navigate those environments while remaining grounded in their identity and calling.

##### Example 3: Threats, Slander, and Danger

The family has experienced online threats, emails, people trying to show up at offices, and weapons being taken from individuals at events. They know the danger but also see lives being changed, creating a balanced rather than fearful posture.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do set firm boundaries for family members who cannot handle the intensity of public scrutiny
- ✅ Do teach children a theology of persecution so they understand rather than merely endure it
- ✅ Also do acknowledge both the danger and the ministry fruit rather than ignoring either
- ❌ Don't force family members to engage with public criticism or social media if it harms them
- ❌ Don't let fear completely shut down ministry engagement without also weighing the cost of inaction

#### Keep In Mind

- Family members have to live with the consequences of public ministry even if they are not in the pulpit
- Teaching children to pray for persecutors rather than hate them protects their own hearts
- The family sees people being healed and lives impacted, which balances the weight of threats

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Threats have come via online messages, emails, and in-person attempts to approach offices and gathering locations
- Weapons including knives and guns have been intercepted at events
- Family members must be prepared for the reality that public ministry exposes the entire household to risk

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Exposing family members to the full brunt of social media harassment and online threats
**Solution:** Set boundaries such as removing family members from social media and teaching private coping strategies

**Problem:** Allowing fear of threats to paralyze ministry decisions without considering the cost of not ministering
**Solution:** Weigh both the danger and the fruit, and let legal counsel and governing boards help inform safety decisions

### Topic 5

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Make a Vow to God to Preach Faithfully

Commit before God to preach whatever the Holy Spirit tells you, regardless of series length, topic, or popular reaction. This vow provides an anchor when criticism rises and removes the need to please people rather than faithfully proclaim the text.

##### Step 2: Prepare for Suffering as Part of Faithful Ministry

Accept that if you preach the text faithfully, you will share in Christ's sufferings as well as His blessings. Navigate criticism on your knees and in tears. Do not be surprised when proclamation agitates camps and ruffles feathers.

##### Step 3: Ground Your Urgency in Biblical Truth, Not Sensationalism

Let the reality that every day brings us closer to Christ's return drive your urgency, not sensationalism. Study Revelation and the seven churches with historical and archaeological depth to awaken the body of Christ to what they are not paying attention to.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Multi-Year Preaching Commitments

Philip has made vows to God to preach through entire books or sections of Scripture for extended periods: Matthew for three years, then Acts, then Ephesians. This long obedience in the same direction builds credibility and depth rather than chasing audience preferences.

##### Example 2: "Time Is Running Out" as Biblical, Not Sensational

Philip emphasizes that saying time is running out is not sensationalism but biblical. Because every day that passes brings us closer to Christ's return, urgency is simply a faithful reading of Scripture rather than a manipulative tactic.

##### Example 3: Five-Year Battle with Depression

Philip's book "Contend" includes a chapter on his five-year battle with depression, which he has never shared publicly before. This vulnerability demonstrates that faithful ministry does not exempt leaders from personal suffering, and transparency about that suffering can strengthen ministry credibility.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do make a vow to God to preach faithfully regardless of audience reaction
- ✅ Do accept suffering as part of faithful ministry rather than a sign that you are doing something wrong
- ✅ Also do ground urgency for evangelism and discipleship in biblical eschatology, not sensationalism
- ❌ Don't change preaching content based on criticism or a desire to avoid controversy
- ❌ Don't avoid speaking about Christ's return because it feels uncomfortable or culturally irrelevant

#### Keep In Mind

- Philip's book "Contend" releases October 13th and includes unprecedented personal vulnerability
- Studying Revelation and the seven churches in Turkey provided archaeological and historical depth to his eschatological teaching
- The vow to preach whatever the Holy Spirit says drives all ministry movements and decisions

#### Security & Safety Notes

- None specific to this topic

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Changing preaching content to avoid criticism or controversy
**Solution:** Anchor yourself in a vow to God to preach faithfully, and accept criticism as a natural result of faithful proclamation

**Problem:** Using eschatological urgency as sensationalism rather than biblical faithfulness
**Solution:** Ground urgency in the clear biblical truth that every day brings us closer to Christ's return, and study Scripture with historical and archaeological depth

### Topic 6

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Evaluate Material Desires Against Experience

Test your desire for luxury by exposing yourself to it. Philip worked as a valet in Ohio driving luxury cars, and after that experience those things no longer moved him. Practical exposure can reveal whether material things hold genuine appeal or imagined appeal.

##### Step 2: Simplify Your Lifestyle Intentionally

Adopt a simple, consistent wardrobe and spending habits. Philip wears mostly black shirts and jeans and only spends money on sneakers. A low-maintenance spouse and shared contentment with material things create freedom to focus on ministry rather than acquisition.

##### Step 3: Direct Prayer and Energy Toward Ministry and Mission, Not Grandeur

Pray for resources to take the gospel to specific mission fields South Africa, Middle East, Netherlands, and Europe but not for personal grandeur. Let your rumination be about the condition of your heart, the purity of your team, fires to put out as staff grows, the condition of the body of Christ, and souls being saved.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Valet Experience in Ohio

Philip's time driving luxury cars as a valet in Ohio gave him firsthand exposure to wealth and status symbols. After experiencing that world, material things lost their grip on his desires, freeing him to live simply as a pastor.

##### Example 2: Black Shirts and Sneakers

Philip's personal style is intentionally minimal: mostly black shirts and jeans, with sneakers as his only real indulgence. His wife is low-maintenance. Together they are very content people who do not spend energy on material acquisition.

##### Example 3: Prayer for Mission Fields Over Personal Luxury

Philip prays that God would provide resources to take the gospel to South Africa, the Middle East, the Netherlands, and Europe for crusades. The resources are sought for mission expansion, not personal comfort or status.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do test your material desires through real exposure rather than imagining they will satisfy
- ✅ Do intentionally simplify your lifestyle to free up energy and resources for ministry
- ✅ Also do direct your rumination and prayer toward the condition of your heart, team purity, and souls being saved
- ❌ Don't let material desires drive decisions when you have already seen their emptiness
- ❌ Don't pray for personal grandeur even while asking God for ministry resources

#### Keep In Mind

- Philip's strongest ministry part is not proclamation but the prayer ministry people do not see
- His heritage of prayer comes from his mother, aunts, and family prayer warriors
- Contentment is not passive; it is actively choosing to focus on spiritual rather than material priorities

#### Security & Safety Notes

- None specific to this topic

#### Common Pitfalls

**Problem:** Accumulating wealth or status symbols as ministry success grows
**Solution:** Expose yourself to luxury early so you understand its emptiness, and intentionally adopt a simple lifestyle that protects your contentment

**Problem:** Ruminating over material lack rather than spiritual or ministry needs
**Solution:** Train your thoughts to dwell on the condition of your heart, your team's purity, and souls being saved rather than what you do not own
