>> 1) When Philip entered the sanctuary in 2019, he saw what appeared to be a shift from something once sacred into the idolatry of a leader. The altar was becoming defiled and he perceived a warning concerning mixture and idolatry entering the altar. He has received threats online, threats and emails, people have tried to show up at offices. They've taken knives and guns off people. His wife no longer goes on social media. His children still have to be in school and spaces where his name comes up. They have to navigate that. There's been threats, slander, and they know the danger. But they also see lives being impacted, people being healed, so they see the balance. Philip tells his children never to hate those people, don't fight back on social media, pray for them, because Jesus said if they hated him first they would hate us, and there's a blessing for being wrongly persecuted. He trains them to be prayerful towards those who persecute their father and not retaliate or slander.

>> 2) Security ramp up: He is a very normal person who still walks the streets. But the church now is at a different place and they have attorneys and legal structures that require security when leaving Atlanta or being in certain spaces. It's not a flex, it's legally required by board of directors and attorneys. There have been credible threats online, in emails, letters, people trying to show up at offices and gathering locations. They've taken knives and guns off people. Somebody tried to approach him in the pulpit and grab him. This happened maybe three times in the last couple years. There have been credible threats in different cities when he travels. He acquiesces to legal counsel for safety and to go home to his family. He is a steward, not an owner, and submits to the governing people of the ministry.

>> 3) Street Preachers podcast tour pricing: People push back about fees and VIP prices asking why a pastor is charging. First he was frustrated and angry. He listened and understands the concern. 2019 church does access for free with no offering. The Street Preachers podcast is a completely separate entity from 2019, powered by disciples with resources. Tim Timberlake's vision was to tour. Ticket prices are not to make them rich - they pay for venues, worship leaders, travel, hotels, insurance, production. Tickets are around $60. There are two categories of critiques: those who say if it's the gospel why charge (fair critique), and those who just hate him no matter what. The only way to avoid criticism is to do nothing. He has to accept that if he preaches the text faithfully, it will ruffle feathers and agitate camps. He has made vows to God to preach whatever the Holy Spirit tells him to, whether that's Matthew for 3 years, then Acts, then Ephesians. He navigates criticism on his knees and in tears. He accepts he must share in Christ's sufferings as well as his blessings.

>> 4) Slander in the church: His book "Contend" has a chapter where he talks about hearing his four kids fight when they were little, and he would listen to see if they could resolve conflict. It broke his heart to hear them saying things about each other when they had the same father and mother. He put this in his book because his pet peeve is how the body of Christ slanders one another on social media. He asks: if an imperfect father felt grieved listening to his children demonize each other, how does the heavenly father feel when he sees his children constantly attacking one another? Social media has given everyone a platform for an opinion. There are times to call out wrong, but most of the time people should pray for those they have issues with or talk to them in private rather than publicly slandering. He gives an example of a close friend who is journeying in faith - he posted wedding pics and Christians attacked his wife's dress in the comments, causing the friend to ask "is this why people don't want to go to church?" Philip said the church is a beautiful place but a glorious mess, and this self-righteous behavior is exactly why people stay away. He says the storefront preacher burdened to see Jesus preached is thanking God for Philip because he can't reach 80,000 people at AT&T Stadium.

>> 5) Book "Contend" drops October 13th. It includes a chapter on his 5-year battle with depression that he has never shared publicly before. He talks about archaeological and historical insights he's gained from studying Revelation and the seven churches in Turkey. He wants to awaken the body of Christ to what they're not paying attention to and prepare them for Christ's return. He says every day that passes we're closer to Christ's return, so "time is running out" is not sensationalism - it's biblical. He has made a vow to God to faithfully preach whatever the Holy Spirit tells him, and that drives all his movements.

>> 6) Personal lifestyle: He doesn't have material desires. He worked valet in Ohio driving luxury cars and after experiencing that, those things don't move him. He wears mostly black shirts and jeans, only spends money on sneakers. His wife is low-maintenance. They're very content people. He ruminate over the condition of his heart, the purity of his team, fires to put out as staff grows, the condition of the body of Christ, and souls being saved - not material things. He prays God would provide resources to take the gospel to South Africa, Middle East, Netherlands, Europe for crusades, but not for personal grandeur.

>> 7) Prayer warriors: His strongest part of ministry is not proclamation but what people don't see. His heritage of prayer comes from his mother, aunts, and family prayer warriors. They have a robust prayer team at church led by Rhonda Jackson who has been with him 15 years. Staff cannot have a meeting without prayer. He gives them prayer requests every week. He has a small circle of brothers he reaches out to when struggling. They keep him accountable.
