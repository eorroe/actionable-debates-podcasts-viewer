Debate can be about learning. Argument can be about winning.

Debate is driven by curiosity and reason. It is about exchanging ideas, testing assumptions, and pursuing clarity. Debate may be passionate, but it remains respectful and fact-based.

Reframe culture around debate. Welcome disagreement, separate ideas from people, and keep discussions fact-based.

Diverse perspectives lead to better strategies.

When people feel safe to share opposing views, loyalty grows.

Focus on issues instead of personalities, debate prevents conflict.

Ideas improve when debated, not when dismissed by arguments.

Model calm, respectful debate. If you resort to arguments—or shut down conversations—your people will do the same.

Separate people from ideas. Critique the idea, not the individual.

Ask clarifying questions. Show curiosity, even when you disagree.

We need courage to engage with people who see things differently—and to do so with respect and conviction.

Debate can strengthen. Argument can divide.

Debates are formal, structured exchanges driven by curiosity and reason, whereas arguments are emotionally driven.
