# Holy or Hollow | Street Preachers | Philip Anthony Mitchell & Tim Timberlake

## Overview

This podcast challenges listeners to distinguish authentic Christianity from hollow imitation by pursuing true holiness rather than polished but powerless faith. Tim Timberlake and Philip Anthony Mitchell walk through Scripture—specifically 1 Peter—to confront the deception of a polished but powerless faith and call believers to lives genuinely transformed by the gospel.

## When to Follow Podcast

- When you feel your faith is being shaped more by culture than by Jesus
- When you want to evaluate whether your Christianity is authentic or merely performative
- When you need to pursue true holiness in a world obsessed with appearances
- When you are ready to stop settling for a veneer of Christianity and embrace genuine gospel transformation

## Discussed

### Steward Your Time Intentionally

Time is a fixed resource that God has entrusted to you. You have exactly 1,440 minutes each day, and you do not own them—you steward them. Life is brief, described in Scripture as a vapor or a blade of grass that springs up in the morning and is gone by evening. Because you do not know how much time you have left, you must invest it in God-glorifying activities and relationships that align with His eternal purposes rather than wasting it on distractions that will not last.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Acknowledge that time is fixed.

Accept that you will not get another day, month, or decade beyond what God intends. You do not control the length of your life.
##### Step 2: Audit how you spend your 1,440 minutes.

Look honestly at your daily schedule and identify where time is going to activities and distractions that do not advance God's kingdom.
##### Step 3: Eliminate time-wasting activities.

Stop giving your days to meetings, relationships, and pursuits that do not bear eternal fruit.
##### Step 4: Be intentional with relationships.

Intentionally invest in your spouse, children, staff, church, and community.
##### Step 5: Align your calendar with eternal value.

Prioritize activities that reflect what will matter when you stand before God.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The NBA Player

NBA player Brandon Clark died suddenly at age 27, illustrating that no one knows how much time they have left. Because time can end unexpectedly, every day should be treated as a gift to be invested wisely.

##### Example 2: The Book Author

Tim Timberlake was working on his book "Contend" and packed it with as much content as possible because he did not know if he would get a second book. He wrote like "a man running out of time," name-dropping mentors, family, and lessons because he wanted to ensure nothing important was left unsaid.

##### Example 3: Cleaning Bathrooms to the Glory of God

A new believer cleaned church bathrooms while listening to worship music, making them immaculate. Whether cleaning toilets or preaching the gospel, the standard is excellence because everything is done as unto the Lord.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Treat every day as a non-renewable resource—you cannot get it back.
- ✅ Write down what you want to be known for at the end of your life and let that guide your daily choices.
- ✅ Invest intentionally in your spouse, children, and local church.
- ✅ Work with excellence whether your boss is a believer or not.
- ✅ Give away time only to people and pursuits that bear eternal fruit.

#### Keep In Mind

- Time is a stewardship, not something you own. God, who lives outside of time, has locked humanity between two boundaries, from Adam's beginning to Christ's return, and called you to steward it.
- How you steward your time dictates and determines how and where you will spend eternity. This gives new meaning to the phrase "the test of time."

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share sensitive personal or family information publicly when discussing your time stewardship journey.
- If you are in a dangerous or abusive situation, seek help from trusted authorities and counselors rather than making major life changes alone.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Focusing only on immediate comfort and neglecting eternal consequences.
  **Solution:** Periodically step back and view your life from the perspective of eternity. Ask yourself, "Will this matter when I stand before God?"
- **Problem:** Wasting time because you assume you have plenty of years ahead.
  **Solution:** Remind yourself daily that life is brief and fixed. Plan and act as if you could have fewer years than you expect.
- **Problem:** Being late, leaving early, or stealing from your employer because your boss is unbelieving.
  **Solution:** Work as unto the Lord regardless of your circumstances. Excellence is your witness.

### Live With an Eternal Perspective

Too many believers fall into a myopic view that sees only the present moment—their job, their comfort, their immediate circumstances. They miss the bigger picture of what God is doing in history and where their life fits into eternity. To live well, you must keep one eye on your current responsibilities and both eyes on eternity. Stepping back from the immediacy of now allows you to see the details of how God desires to prepare you for the coming of His Son.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Step back from your current circumstances.

Intentionally create space to see beyond your immediate situation.
##### Step 2: Visualize eternity.

Daily remind yourself that this life is temporary and that you are moving toward a never-ending existence with God or apart from Him.
##### Step 3: Ask the "end of life" question.

Determine what you want to be known for when you take your last breath. If you had to limit it to one word, what would it be?
##### Step 4: Align daily decisions with the end goal.

Let your final breath—and what you want it to say—shape how you live today.
##### Step 5: Let the Holy Spirit work in the gap between now and your next.

Allow God to weave together even the broken seasons of your life for His glory.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Nose on the Mirror

If you stand too close to a mirror with your nose pressed against it, you cannot see the details of your face well enough to apply makeup or fix your hair. You must step back to see clearly. The same is true spiritually: if you are too close to "now," you cannot see eternity or how God is preparing you.

##### Example 2: The Skyline of New York City

If you stare only at your immediate circumstances, you miss the vast plan of redemption God is orchestrating. Stepping back allows you to see both your current situation and the bigger picture at the same time.

##### Example 3: Wesley and Whitfield

John Wesley and George Whitfield traveled on horseback across Europe and America, taking boats across the sea, enduring long horseback rides and boat voyages across continents to spread the gospel. They saw the bigger picture of eternity and were willing to sacrifice comfort for the spread of the message. Today, with cars, planes, and resources, what is our excuse for not doing the same?

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Set aside time each day to view your life through the lens of eternity.
- ✅ Ask yourself, "What do I want to be known for when I die?" and let that one word shape your decisions.
- ✅ Read Scripture about eternity and the return of Christ to keep the long view alive in your heart.
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who challenge you to see beyond the present.

#### Keep In Mind

- You cannot see the details of how God is preparing you if you are standing too close to now. Distance from the immediate gives you perspective on the eternal.
- The church needs bold voices to wake people up to the bigger picture of what is really going on in these final days of the church age.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not make major life decisions based solely on emotional reactions to eternity. Seek wise counsel and prayerful discernment.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being so fixed on current comfort that you miss God's call to sacrifice and spread the gospel.
  **Solution:** Periodically remind yourself of the eternal reward and the souls that need to hear the message. Ask God to expand your vision.
- **Problem:** Myopic focus on personal problems or career advancement.
  **Solution:** Intentionally step back and ask, "What is God doing in the bigger picture, and how does my life fit into it?"
- **Problem:** Assuming that because you are comfortable now, you are where God wants you.
  **Solution:** Comfort can be a trap. Evaluate whether your comfort is hindering the spread of the gospel or your own spiritual growth.

### Obey God's Promptings and Die Empty

Believers sit on what God told them to do in a previous season while asking for new assignments in the current season. The call is to obey what God has already told you to do, be faithful and fruitful with it, and live in such a way that you die empty—having poured out everything God placed in you. When you stand before the Lord, you want to say, "I fought the good fight, I kept the faith, I finished my race," and hear, "Well done, good and faithful servant."

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Identify the last thing God told you to do that you have not yet obeyed.

This includes a relationship to reconcile, a person to apologize to, a work to go back and fix, or a ministry to start.
##### Step 2: Repent of sitting on God's instructions.

Confess that you have been unfaithful with what He has already given you.
##### Step 3: Take immediate action.

Obedience does not require perfect conditions—it requires a willing heart. Start today.
##### Step 4: Be faithful in the small assignments.

Love your spouse, love your children, disciple them, give a portion of your income to gospel ministry, and serve your local church faithfully.
##### Step 5: Live like a man or woman running out of time.

Write, speak, serve, and give as if you may not get another opportunity.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Man Running Out of Time

Tim Timberlake packed his first book with as much content as possible because he did not know if he would get a second book. He included mentors, family, and lessons because he felt the urgency of his finite time. This is the mindset every believer should have.

##### Example 2: Die Empty

The hosts encourage people to "die empty"—to pour out everything God placed in them rather than arriving in glory with unfinished work. This means using your gifts, resources, and opportunities now. When you arrive in glory, God will not need the sermons you preached, the songs you sang, or the podcast you made; those are for now.

##### Example 3: The Drink Offering

Paul described his life being offered up like a drink offering—poured out completely. The call is to be emptied and obedient down to the last drop so that your life is meaningful and pleasing to God.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Keep a journal of what God speaks to you and review it regularly to check your obedience.
- ✅ Make amends quickly when you realize you have wronged someone or not obeyed God.
- ✅ Serve faithfully in your local church even when it is inconvenient or unnoticed.
- ✅ Give a portion of your income to support gospel ministry and soul-winning.

#### Keep In Mind

- Obedience now is the prerequisite for new assignments. You cannot ask God for more if you have not been faithful with what He already gave you.
- The things God has given you—your platform, ministry, finances, relationships, and opportunities—are for now. They will not follow you into eternity, but the fruit of your faithfulness will.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share deeply personal details of your spiritual struggles or failures in public forums without discernment. Seek accountability with trusted spiritual leaders instead.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Asking God for a new assignment while ignoring the last thing He told you to do.
  **Solution:** List the things God has already told you to do. Obey those before seeking new directions.
- **Problem:** Waiting for perfect conditions or more time before obeying.
  **Solution:** Obedience does not require ideal circumstances. God calls you to faithfulness now, not later.
- **Problem:** Wanting to die full, holding back gifts, time, or resources for yourself.
  **Solution:** Ask God to reveal what you are withholding. Give it away while you can. Die empty.

### Work As Unto the Lord

Your job, whether it is cleaning toilets or preaching the gospel, is an opportunity to glorify God. The Scripture teaches that you should work as unto the Lord, not unto men. It does not matter if your boss is humble, an unbeliever, or from another religion. Excellence, faithfulness, and integrity in your work are your witness to the world. Being late, leaving early, or stealing from your employer are forms of unfaithfulness that dishonor the God you serve.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Recognize that your job is an assignment from God.

Whether you are at Starbucks, a church, or a corporate office, your work is a stewardship.
##### Step 2: Arrive on time and leave when scheduled.

Do not steal time from your employer.
##### Step 3: Do not take what does not belong to you.

Avoid stealing supplies, padding expenses, or misusing company resources.
##### Step 4: Excellence is your standard.

Clean the bathroom with worship music playing if that is your assignment. Preach with preparation if that is your assignment. The standard is the same: do it as unto the Lord.
##### Step 5: Work even when your boss is unbelieving.

Your witness is magnified when you serve faithfully in difficult environments.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Cleaning Bathrooms

A new believer cleaned church bathrooms while listening to worship music, making them immaculate. He did it to the glory of God, demonstrating that no task is too small when done for the Lord.

##### Example 2: The $100 Analogy

If God gave you only $100 and you would never receive another dollar, you would be careful with how you spend even $1. Yet believers waste hours, days, and months of time as if they have an unlimited supply. Work with the same intentionality.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Arrive early, stay focused, and leave when your shift ends.
- ✅ Offer your work to God in prayer before you begin each day.
- ✅ Keep your word and meet deadlines even when no one is watching.
- ✅ Refuse to complain about your boss; instead, pray for them and serve them well.

#### Keep In Mind

- You do not own anything. Everything under your leadership and within your hands is a gift from God to steward. You will give an account for how you managed it.
- The unknown writer of Hebrews says, "It is appointed to man once to die and then the judgment." No one escapes that account.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- If your job requires you to compromise your faith or ethical standards, prayerfully consider whether you should remain in that position. Seek wise counsel before making a decision.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being unfaithful at work because your boss is unbelieving or difficult.
  **Solution:** Remember that you work as unto the Lord, not unto men. Your faithfulness in difficult places is a powerful testimony.
- **Problem:** Treating work as merely a means to a paycheck rather than a stewardship.
  **Solution:** View every task as an opportunity to glorify God. Excellence is your offering.
- **Problem:** Stealing time or resources from your employer.
  **Solution:** Recognize that dishonesty in your job is unfaithfulness to God. Work with integrity in all things.

### Reconcile Broken Relationships

Before you make a list of all the ministry plans you want to do for God in the future, examine what He has already told you to do in the past. Ask yourself: what relationship do you need to reconcile? Who do you need to apologize to? What work do you need to go back and fix? Unresolved conflict and unrepented sin block your ability to move forward in faithfulness. Reconciliation is not optional for the believer—it is a mark of genuine Christianity.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Identify the broken relationship.

Who has God placed on your heart that you need to reach out to?
##### Step 2: Take responsibility for your part.

Whether you were fully at fault or not, humble yourself and apologize where needed.
##### Step 3: Go back and fix what you can.

If there is work you abandoned or a situation you left incomplete, prayerfully consider how to make it right.
##### Step 4: Forgive those who have wronged you.

Reconciliation requires both repentance and forgiveness.
##### Step 5: Restore trust through consistent action.

One conversation is not enough. Let your changed life be the proof of your repentance.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Laundry List

The hosts warn against having a list of future ministry plans while ignoring the one thing God told you to do last season. Before asking God for new assignments, resolve what is already on your heart.

##### Example 2: Katherine's Testimony

Katherine Kman said she wants to stand before the Lord and say, "Lord, I tried. I gave it my best." This is the heart of reconciliation—not perfection, but a life marked by effort and humility to make things right.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do not let the sun go down on your anger. Address conflict quickly.
- ✅ Write down who you need to reconcile with and take steps this week.
- ✅ Be specific in your apologies. Do not make excuses or shift blame.
- ✅ Forgive freely, even when the other person has not asked for it.

#### Keep In Mind

- Relationships are gifts from God, and mismanaging them is a form of unfaithful stewardship. How you treat people is one of the clearest evidences of your faith.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- If reconciliation would put you in physical or emotional danger, seek counsel from a pastor or trusted leader before proceeding. Do not compromise your safety for the sake of a relationship.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Focusing on future ministry while ignoring current broken relationships.
  **Solution:** Make a list of unresolved conflicts and address them before pursuing new spiritual projects.
- **Problem:** Pride preventing you from apologizing first.
  **Solution:** Remember that humility is strength. Taking the first step toward reconciliation reflects the heart of Christ.
- **Problem:** Forgiving without restoring appropriate boundaries.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness does not always mean trusting someone immediately. Set wise boundaries while you forgive.

### Give to Gospel Ministry

God has called believers to use a portion of their income to support the spread of the gospel. This is not optional; it is a stewardship. Your money is a tool to gain eternal friends—people who will greet you in heaven and say, "Because of your faithfulness and giving, I am here." Giving is not about the amount; it is about the heart behind it. When you give, you are investing in souls that will last forever.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Determine a portion of your income to give.

Commit to giving a portion of your income to support gospel ministry.
##### Step 2: Choose where to give.

Support local churches, missionaries, and ministries that are faithfully proclaiming the gospel.
##### Step 3: Give cheerfully and regularly.

Make giving a habit, not a one-time event.
##### Step 4: View giving as an investment in eternity.

Every dollar given to advance the gospel is stored up as eternal reward.
##### Step 5: Do not withhold what you have committed to give.

If God has placed an amount or mission on your heart, follow through.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: Gaining Friends in Heaven

Jesus taught that you can use your money to gain friends in heaven. People will meet you in glory and say, "Because of your faithfulness and giving, I am here." This is the eternal return on your investment.

##### Example 2: The Power of 1440

The hosts reference Tim Timberlake's book, *The Power of 1440*, which teaches that stewarding time—and by extension all resources—is a matter of eternal significance. Giving your time and money to gospel ministry is part of that stewardship.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Give before you spend on yourself. Make giving a first priority, not an afterthought.
- ✅ Support ministries that are rooted in the Word of God and producing fruit.
- ✅ Give secretly and humbly, not for recognition or applause.
- ✅ Review your giving regularly to ensure it reflects your growth and increased resources.

#### Keep In Mind

- Everything you have belongs to God. Giving is not generosity; it is simply returning to Him what is already His. The question is whether you are being a faithful steward of what He has entrusted to you.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Research ministries thoroughly before giving large sums. Ensure they are legitimate, transparent, and aligned with biblical teaching.
- Do not give under emotional manipulation or pressure. Giving should be a decision made with a clear mind and a willing heart.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Giving only what is left over after all other spending.
  **Solution:** Make giving a first priority. Give as unto the Lord, not as an afterthought.
- **Problem:** Giving to be seen or praised by others.
  **Solution:** Give secretly. Let God be your reward. Matthew 6 teaches that the Father who sees in secret will reward you.
- **Problem:** Withholding giving because of doubt about how the money will be used.
  **Solution:** Trust God with the outcome. Your responsibility is to give faithfully; God will honor your obedience.

### Define Success by Eternal Metrics

Culture defines success by attendance numbers, financial growth, platforms, and comfort. But the believer's definition of success must align with Paul's final words: "I have fought the good fight, I have kept the faith, I have finished my race." Success is not measured by how many people attend your church or how much money you leave for your children. It is measured by whether you lived a life that pleased God, poured out for Christ, and finished the race He set before you.

#### Steps

##### Step 1: Redefine success by Paul's words.

Let "I fought the good fight, I kept the faith, I finished my race" be your standard, not attendance goals or financial milestones.
##### Step 2: Identify your race.

God has given each person a calling. Do not compare your race to anyone else's.
##### Step 3: Live with your deathbed in view.

Picture your final moments and ask, "Will I be able to say I finished my race with confidence?"
##### Step 4: Reject cultural metrics of success.

Platforms, performance, and appearances are hollow. God has never been impressed by what merely looks spiritual.
##### Step 5: Pursue love as your legacy.

If you had to be known for one word, let it be love. Every decision should flow from that desire.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: The Encounter in Jerusalem

Philip Anthony Mitchell had a transformative encounter with God at the Garden Tomb in Jerusalem in 2018. That encounter shifted his definition of success from attendance goals and financial security to finishing his race well and being known for love.

##### Example 2: John Wesley and George Whitfield

These revivalists traveled on horseback across continents and by boat across the sea, enduring long horseback rides and boat voyages to spread the gospel because they saw the bigger picture of eternity. Their success was not measured by comfort but by souls reached and lives transformed.

##### Example 3: The Crown Laid Up

Paul wrote that the crown of righteousness was laid up for him because he finished his race. This eternal reward is the true metric of success, not the temporary applause of this world.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Write down your definition of success and revisit it regularly.
- ✅ Compare your life against Scripture, not social media or cultural standards.
- ✅ Ask trusted spiritual leaders to hold you accountable to eternal metrics.
- ✅ Choose one word you want to be known for and align all your decisions with it.

#### Keep In Mind

- Culture celebrates image, platforms reward performance, and the Church has settled for a faith measured by attendance goals, financial growth, and leaving money for children rather than by finishing your race well. God has never been impressed by what merely looks spiritual. Authentic transformation is what matters.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share your personal struggles with success or failure in public spaces without wisdom. Seek accountability in trusted, safe relationships.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Measuring success by attendance, financial growth, or platform size.
  **Solution:** Anchor your definition of success in Scripture, specifically Paul's words about finishing his race well.
- **Problem:** Comparing your race to someone else's ministry or life.
  **Solution:** Focus on your own race. God has given you a calling. Run it well.
- **Problem:** Seeking to be known for your preaching, platform, or leadership rather than for love.
  **Solution:** Ask yourself, "What do I want to be known for when I die?" If the answer is not love, realign your priorities.
